## I. The Definition and Hazards of Time Paradoxes

A time paradox refers to the phenomenon in which, during a time regression operation, causal logic becomes self-contradictory due to interference behavior. Time paradoxes are the most dangerous consequence of time regression operations; once they occur and are not repaired in time, they may trigger timeline splits, causal chain collapse, and even cosmic-level law tremors. Therefore, the time paradox prevention and control mechanism is the most core and critical component of the entire time regression safety system.

The essence of a time paradox is "causal closure failure". In a normal flow of time, every event has its causes and effects, and the causal chain presents a continuous linear structure. A time regression operation introduces a variable from the future into a past time period; if the behavior of this variable changes the causal conditions that led to its own existence, a "causal closed-loop rupture" is formed — that is, the logical contradiction that if A occurs, then A will not occur. At the level of laws, this contradiction manifests as a violent conflict of causal energy; if the conflicting energy cannot be absorbed by the law network, it will erupt in the form of a time paradox.

The degree of hazard of a time paradox depends on its scale and location of occurrence. Small-scale paradoxes (such as changing an ordinary person's daily choices) may only cause local causal fluctuations, which the law network can absorb and repair on its own; medium-scale paradoxes (such as changing the fate of an important historical figure) may cause timeline branching, forming parallel timelines; large-scale paradoxes (such as changing the direction of a civilization-level historical event) may trigger causal chain collapse, leading to the collapse of the law structure of the entire timeline. The goal of the Hengyuan Universe's time paradox prevention and control mechanism is to eliminate paradoxes in their infancy, ensuring that no time regression operation triggers a medium-scale or larger paradox.

## II. The Classification of Time Paradoxes

According to the formation mechanism and manifestation of paradoxes, time paradoxes are divided into the following four major categories:

| Paradox Type | Formation Mechanism | Typical Case | Hazard Level |
|----------|----------|----------|----------|
| Parricide Paradox | The retrogressor's behavior leads to the elimination of the causal conditions of his own existence | The retrogressor kills his own ancestor in the past, causing himself never to be born | Extremely High (Red) |
| Information Paradox | Information is transmitted from the future to the past, received and developed by people in the past, and eventually becomes the source of the future information, forming a sourceless information loop | A future scientist transmits a theorem to the past, a past scientist publishes the theorem, and the future scientist precisely learned the theorem from historical literature | High (Orange) |
| Ontological Paradox | The retrogressor meets his past self in the past, and two existences of the same ontology interact, causing causal confusion in the ontology's consciousness | The retrogressor meets and talks with his younger self in the past, changing the younger self's life choices, which in turn changes the retrogressor's own memories and personality | High (Orange) |
| Event Paradox | The retrogressor's behavior changes the outcome of a key historical event, requiring the causal chain of the entire subsequent history to be reconstructed | The retrogressor prevents the outbreak of a key war in the past, causing the subsequent hundreds of years of history to change completely | Extremely High (Red) |

In addition to the above four major categories, there are also some composite paradoxes — complex paradoxes that simultaneously contain the characteristics of more than two paradox types. The prevention, control, and repair of composite paradoxes are far more difficult than those of single-type paradoxes, requiring the highest level of emergency response.

## III. The Pre-operation Prevention and Control Mechanism

The primary strategy of time paradox prevention and control is "pre-operation prevention" — eliminating the possibility of paradox generation through institutional and technical means before the time regression operation begins. The pre-operation prevention and control mechanism includes the following core links:

### 3.1 Pre-regression Causal Assessment

Before any time regression operation is approved, a professional team of "causal analysts" must conduct a pre-regression causal assessment. The assessment content includes: the causal chain density of the target time period, the location and sensitivity of key historical nodes, the degree of causal correlation between the retrogressor himself and the target time period, and the scope of causal deviation that the proposed interference behavior may cause. The assessment results are expressed as a "paradox risk index"; regression applications with an index higher than 0.1 will be rejected, and those with an index between 0.01 and 0.1 can only be approved after additional prevention and control measures are attached.

### 3.2 Causal Isolation of the Retrogressor

To prevent the occurrence of the parricide paradox and the ontological paradox, the retrogressor must undergo "causal isolation treatment" before entering the target time period. The core of causal isolation is, through Anchor law operations, temporarily "detaching" the retrogressor's causal imprint from the causal chain of one's own plane, so that the retrogressor's existence in the target time period does not have a direct association with his own past causal line. After causal isolation, even if the retrogressor encounters his own ancestor or his past self in the past, he will not directly trigger the parricide paradox or the ontological paradox — because the retrogressor's existence has been temporarily made independent of the original causal chain.

The validity period of causal isolation is consistent with the duration of the regression; after the regression ends, the retrogressor's causal imprint will be reconnected to the original causal chain. At the time of reconnection, a "causal continuity verification" is required to ensure that the experiences during the regression period can be seamlessly connected with the retrogressor's original memories.

### 3.3 The Whitelist System for Interference Behaviors

Interference behaviors in time regression implement a strict "whitelist system" — only interference behaviors that have been approved in advance and included in the whitelist are permitted to be executed, and any interference behavior outside the whitelist is strictly prohibited. The whitelist is formulated based on the results of the pre-regression causal assessment, containing only those interference behaviors with extremely small causal deviation and extremely low paradox risk.

Typical examples of whitelisted interference behaviors include: placing a piece of information in an unnoticed corner, conducting a brief exchange with a non-critical person that does not affect his life trajectory, and making an extremely minor time adjustment (not exceeding a few seconds) to an event that is about to occur naturally. Any interference behavior that may change the fate of key figures, the outcome of key events, or the direction of civilizational evolution must never be included in the whitelist.

## IV. In-operation Monitoring and Emergency Response

Even with strict pre-operation prevention, unexpected situations may still occur during the regression process that raise the paradox risk. Therefore, in-operation monitoring and emergency response form the second line of defense of the prevention and control mechanism.

### 4.1 Real-Time Causal Monitoring

During the regression operation, the Anchor network performs real-time monitoring of the causal chain state of the target time period. The monitoring indicators include: the rate of change of causal chain density, the stability of key nodes, and paradox precursor signals (abnormal fluctuations of causal energy). The monitoring data is updated every millisecond and automatically analyzed by the "Causal Monitoring AI"; any anomaly triggers an immediate alarm.

### 4.2 Three-Level Emergency Response

According to the severity of the paradox risk, the emergency response is divided into three levels:

- **Level 3 Response (Warning Level)**: monitoring detects slight anomalies in the causal chain, and the paradox risk index rises above 0.01 but below 0.05. Response measures: suspend the current interference behavior, the retrogressor falls back to a pure observation state, and the causal analyst team analyzes and assesses the anomaly; operations may continue only after safety is confirmed.

- **Level 2 Response (Alert Level)**: the causal chain anomaly intensifies, the paradox risk index rises above 0.05 but below 0.1, or clear paradox precursor signals appear. Response measures: immediately terminate all interference behaviors, initiate the retrogressor emergency withdrawal procedure, and simultaneously have the Anchor network perform "causal freezing" on the causal chain of the target time period — temporarily locking the state of the causal chain to prevent further spread of the anomaly.

- **Level 1 Response (Crisis Level)**: a paradox has actually occurred, or the paradox risk index exceeds 0.1. Response measures: immediately initiate the highest-level emergency response, with the Time Law Committee directing directly, mobilizing all available Anchor energy to conduct "causal repair" operations, while preparing to initiate the "timeline isolation" procedure — isolating the time segment affected by the paradox from the main timeline to form an independent closed time loop, preventing the paradox's influence from spreading to other parts of the main timeline.

## V. Post-operation Causal Repair and Audit

If a paradox has already occurred, post-operation causal repair is the final remedial measure. The core method of causal repair is the "causal energy injection method" — injecting precisely calculated compensatory energy into the damaged causal chain through the Anchor network to offset the causal conflict caused by the paradox, restoring the causal chain to stability. The repair process requires extremely high precision; the deviation of injected energy must not exceed 10 to the power of negative 6 of the target energy, otherwise a secondary paradox may be triggered.

For paradoxes that have already led to timeline branching, the repair method is "timeline merging" — re-merging the branch timeline back into the main timeline through Anchor operations. During the merging process, the differing events in the branch timeline need to be "causally overwritten", that is, the original events of the main timeline overwrite the branch events, ultimately retaining only one unified timeline.

After all time regression operations end, a "post-operation audit of time operations" must be accepted. The audit is responsible by the Time Law Audit Committee, and its content includes: whether the regression operation was strictly executed according to the approved scheme, whether there were interference behaviors outside the whitelist, whether the causal monitoring data is complete, and whether paradox risks occurred and were handled appropriately. The audit reports are permanently archived as important references for subsequent regression approvals.

## Conclusion

The time paradox prevention and control mechanism is the most precise and complex safety project of the Hengyuan Universe in the field of time laws. With pre-operation prevention as its foundation, in-operation monitoring as its shield, and post-operation repair as its bottom line, it constructs a full-chain prevention and control system of "prevention — monitoring — response — repair — audit". The existence of this mechanism enables time regression, a powerful and dangerous law ability, to be safely used for archaeology and research, without becoming a Pandora's box that destroys the causal order of the universe.
