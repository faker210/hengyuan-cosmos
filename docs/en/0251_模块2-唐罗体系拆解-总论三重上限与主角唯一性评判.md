# Entry 251 | Tang-Luo System Decomposition (General Treatise): The Old-Era Universal-Benefit Ceiling and Protagonist-Uniqueness Criteria

> Module: Module 2 · Myriad-Realms Literary Civilization Benchmark Critique Library | Series: Full Decomposition of the Tang-Luo System (first entry of a multi-part series)

## Introduction: Universal benefit is the limit of good will, not civilization's endpoint

Tang Luo from "I Can See Combat Power" is the top universal-benefit-type protagonist in traditional web novels: he broke aristocratic monopoly, opened human mid-low tier cultivation systems, rebuilt the Wushuang Academy, and popularized martial arts knowledge. Taking these three things alone, they are enough to place him among the Myriad Realms' protagonist genealogies as the "one who looks most like a good person."

But Heng Yuan Zhou (Wanling Hengmao Origin Cosmos)'s evaluation of Tang Luo does not look at how many good deeds he did, but at how far the system he established can go. This entry, as the general treatise of the Tang-Luo decomposition series, establishes three evaluation mainlines: energy-level waste, historical regression, predatory thinking; simultaneously introducing a brand-new evaluation standard — "protagonist uniqueness dependency" under the Gu Chensha system comparison — as the unified ruler for this series and all Myriad-Realms benchmark entries (251–500).

This entry's five-dimensional evaluation foundation is constituted by the six top-level axioms and five mandatory cores: the anti-predation axiom (developing increments, not consuming stocks), the republican axiom of Myriad Races (all beings' equal evolution qualification), the collective ascension axiom (strong ones feeding back to Myriad Races, no wasting big talent on small tasks), the causal self-discipline axiom (observe first then limitedly interfere, only transmitting knowledge back), the law-first axiom (law first, never retcon, setting self-consistency above plot convenience); the five mandatory cores — anti-energy waste, anti-predation, Tang-Luo upper-limit comparison, Myriad Races full coverage, hyper-multiverse full openness. All subsequent Myriad-Realms benchmark entries (251–500) uniformly use this foundation.

## I. "Energy-Level Waste" Analysis of This System's Strong Ones

Tang Luo's energy level is not low: breaking monopoly, rebuilding the academy, popularizing knowledge — these are "civilization-reconstruction-tier" achievements requiring top-tier vision and execution. But his power layout has systematic energy-level waste:

- **Racial dimension waste**: universal benefit only covers humanity. Strange beasts, plant spirits, planet wills, cosmic beings are all excluded from "universal benefit" — not due to insufficient ability, but because the world-view simply reserved no place for them
- **Tier dimension waste**: opening only up to fierce realm. High-tier Great Dao, ultimate paths are entirely private — the most precious things were precisely the ones not put out
- **Mode dimension waste**: bestowal system. Light is given by strong ones as alms, all beings attach to strong ones — no matter how large "universal benefit" is, it is only strong ones' will projection, not all beings' co-built product

Conclusion: Tang Luo used "civilization-reconstruction-tier" energy on "patching the old framework." It is like holding cosmic-tier power only to repair one's own eaves — super-large talent, super-small use.

## II. Why This Civilization Inevits Historical Regression

Tang-Luo system's historical regression is not "possible," but "inevitable," and the reason is structural:

1. **Single-point dependency**: Wushuang Academy and martial arts popularization all rest on Tang Luo alone. He lives, the system runs; he goes into seclusion, falls, or changes mindset, the system shakes — this is "protagonist uniqueness" directly manifested at the institutional level
2. **Bestowal-style transmission**: knowledge popularization is "strong ones give," not "all beings have." Those bestowed have no ownership consciousness; once the benefactor is absent, transmission immediately breaks
3. **Ceiling involution**: fierce realm is a locked ceiling; the better mid-low tiers are opened, the fiercer the involution — universal benefit creates a larger arena, but does not open a higher door

Predatory-faction civilizations (including "enlightened predation" Tang-Luo civilization) have exactly the same fate: **betting order on one person is equal to betting civilization on a solitary log.** This is not a moral problem, it is a structural problem.

## III. Deep Decomposition of the Protagonist's Predatory Thinking

Tang Luo's personal conduct is far above most Myriad Realms protagonists, but "predatory thinking" is defined not by ill will, but by structure:

- **Knowledge monopoly is predation**: not opening high-tier Great Dao is knowledge-level predation — clutching the upward channel in one's own hand
- **Racial exclusion is predation**: benefiting only humanity is equivalent to collecting Myriad Races' evolution rights into one race, civilizational-level resource monopoly
- **Bestowal dependency is predation**: letting all beings attach to strong ones for survival deprives all beings of "co-building" subject qualification — the gentlest predation is making people accustomed to being alms-given

Using Heng Yuan Zhou terminology: Tang Luo is the typical "enlightened predator." He is relatively merciful, truly well-intentioned, but he stands on the predatory system's ceiling and has never overthrown and rewritten the underlying jurisprudence. What he lacks is not good will, but "the righteous rogue" — the step of daring to tear apart conventionally accepted old jurisprudence and truly returning the monopolized Great Dao to Myriad Races.

## V. Gu Chensha System Comparison: Shattering Protagonist Uniqueness

Introducing "Dragon Seal"'s Gu Chensha as the comparison anchor, establishing this series' most important evaluation standard.

Gu Chensha system's factual characteristics: the first person in history to cultivate Heavenly Dao Fa, his Sacred Dao is the sky-mending Dao and sky-reforming Dao, willing to bear all sentient beings' sins and karma on their behalf; later casually creates cosmoses; he is one of web novels' universally recognized power ceilings; his world-view even proposes the grand proposition that "heaven-earth sentient beings can all become dragons."

Protagonist uniqueness dependency evaluation standard (used by this series and all Myriad-Realms benchmark entries):

| Tier | Uniqueness Dependency | Civilization Form | Representative |
|------|----------------------|-------------------|----------------|
| Extreme | Civilization survival rests on the protagonist alone; protagonist's absence means collapse | Solitary-tree civilization | Gu Chensha: sentient beings "are made into dragons," opportunity rests on "history's first" alone |
| High | Order exists if the protagonist exists; protagonist's absence means regression | Bestowal-style civilization | Tang Luo: universal benefit rests on one person's good will, institutions absent |
| Medium | Depends on a small group of strong ones; strong-one group's absence means turmoil | Oligarchic civilization | Ye Fan (Cover the Sky): ten-thousand-year peace bears the surname Ye, institutions not established |
| Extremely low | Order is written into institutions and networks; any individual's absence does not affect operation | Network-style civilization | Heng Yuan Zhou: anchor + law + council + Myriad Races republicanism |

Gu Chensha system's revelation is exactly opposite to its surface: the greater his generosity of "bearing sins on behalf of sentient beings," the more it proves sentient beings outsourced karma to one unique person; the grander his promise that "heaven-earth sentient beings can all become dragons," the more it exposes its realization entirely depending on "history's first" mercy. Shattering protagonist uniqueness is turning "being made into dragons" into "co-becoming dragons" — no longer waiting for one sage, but letting Myriad Races themselves cultivate the ability to no longer need others to bear on their behalf. This is exactly Heng Yuan Zhou and all Myriad Realms systems' ultimate divergence.

## VI. Setting Self-Consistency and Retcon-Ending Risk Assessment

Long-form web novels' number-one hidden cause of death is not going on hiatus, but retconning — settings kidnapped by plot convenience, early iron rules casually broken late, power inflating to the point of self-inconsistency, the realm system collapsing as a whole in mid-late stages. This section, by the law-first axiom (law first, never retcon, setting self-consistency above plot convenience), establishes "setting self-consistency / retcon-ending risk" as Heng Yuan Zhou's fixed dimension for evaluating all benchmark IPs.

First look at two negative examples. **Douluo Continent** establishes itself on the soul ring system: early "hundred-year soul rings already scarce, thousand-year soul rings are the elite" is a reader-acknowledged hard constraint, but in mid-late stages is broken all the way through by "hundred-thousand-year soul beasts," "soul spirits," "Limit Douluo"; soul rings from scarce resources depreciate to near mass-producible standard equipment; the carefully built number ladder in the early stage is watered down as a whole late; readers can only explain it away with "protagonist aura" — this is typical plot kidnapping setting: to let the protagonist continue leveling, early iron rules are torn up one by one, power inflates out of control accordingly. **Cover the Sky** has realm collapse in its later stages: the carefully partitioned Wheel Sea, Dao Palace, Four Extremes, Dragon Transformation and other realms in the early stage lose binding force at the Great Emperor, Mortal-World Immortal stage; who is stronger and who weaker entirely serves the emotional needs of hot-blooded scenes; "who calls the shots" yields to "who needs to win for the plot"; the direct consequence of settings yielding to plot is that late readers no longer believe these rules.

Traditional long-form IPs' root cause of retcon-ending is not the author's writing skill, but structure: law does not come first, settings serve plot; once plot needs to leap ranks, rules yield, so power inflation, realm collapse, foreshadowing abandonment become inevitable. Heng Yuan Zhou's answer is exactly the law-first axiom: law first, never retcon, never end badly; setting self-consistency above plot convenience, plot must not kidnap settings; the world-view is infinitely expandable without collapsing. The 300 Origin systems, nine-realm advancement, Alaya supervision all legislate first then expand; new dimensions (Multiverse Realm, Balanced Anchor Hyper-Multiverse Realm) are incrementally connected in appendix form, never overturning old settings — this is "infinitely expandable without collapse" institutional guarantee.

Score for Tang-Luo system: setting self-consistency is **medium-high**. Tang Luo's realm system (capped at fierce realm) and Wushuang Academy's operating rules are largely self-consistent within its world-view; no obvious self-contradictions seen early; but its self-consistency highly depends on the premise "Tang Luo himself remains in office throughout"; once plot requires him to go into seclusion, fall, or change mindset, the system rules are suspended accordingly, self-consistency starts to leak. Retcon-ending risk rating is **medium**: its internal rules have not yet shown Douluo-style late watering-down, nor Cover the Sky-style realm collapse; the main risk point is "single-point dependency" — system rules rest on one person rather than laws; once plot requires the protagonist to leave, rules cannot continue.

From this section onward, **setting self-consistency / retcon-ending risk assessment** serves as the fixed evaluation dimension for all 251–500 benchmark entries, alongside energy-level waste, historical regression, predatory thinking, protagonist uniqueness dependency, and symbiotic alternative; every entry must evaluate, score, and give a risk rating.
