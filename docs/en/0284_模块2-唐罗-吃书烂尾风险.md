## Introduction: The #1 hidden cause of death for long web novels

Among the six top-level axioms, the law-first axiom requires never retconning, never ending badly. The #1 hidden cause of death for long web novels is not hiatus, but retconning — settings kidnapped by plot convenience, early iron laws casually broken in late stages. This entry takes the law-first axiom as core, specially analyzing the retcon risk in late stages of "I Can See Combat Power," the work where the Tang-Luo system appears. Protagonist uniqueness dependency judgment: **Tang Luo = high dependency / bestowal-style civilization**. The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning. Heng Yuan Zhou's evaluation base has been upgraded to six top-level axioms.

## I. "Energy-Level Waste" Analysis of This System's Strong Ones

Retconning-caused energy-level waste:

- **Early accumulation voided**: numerical ladders, realm rules, world-view iron laws painstakingly built early are broken late. Early foreshadowing, suspense, setup all voided
- **Narrative trust overdrawn**: readers discover rules can be broken, no longer trust the author. Once trust is overdrawn, even good late plot cannot redeem
- **Combat-power inflation out of control**: retconning's direct consequence is combat-power inflation. The protagonist gets stronger and stronger, but readers are numb — because strength depends on the author's mood

## II. Why This Civilization Inevits Historical Regression (Retconning Perspective)

Narrative regression caused by retconning:

1. **Iron laws devalued**: early "hundred-year soul rings already scarce"-style iron laws late become "thousand-year soul rings casually picked up." Iron laws devalued, narrative tension devalues with them
2. **Realm collapse**: realms carefully delineated early lose binding force late. Who is strong or weak depends entirely on plot needs
3. **Foreshadowing unfinished**: suspense laid early has no time to resolve late, can only end badly. Readers' expectations are betrayed

## III. Deep Decomposition of the Protagonist's Predatory Thinking

Predatory thinking behind retconning:

- **Plot kidnaps setting**: for plot climaxes, the author may tear up any early iron law. This is predation on reader intelligence
- **Combat-power inflation**: the protagonist's strength stretches with plot needs, early "leveling hard-won" is diluted by late "casual breakthrough"
- **Settings as tools**: settings are not world-view skeleton, but plot tools. Tools worn out get replaced — this is disrespect to settings themselves

## Supplementary Discussion: Institutional Prevention of Retcon Risk

The Tang-Luo system's late-overturning-early retcon-ending risk roots in the absence of the law-first axiom's rigid constraint. Heng Yuan Zhou by writing core laws into the charter and having the Alaya Network auto-verify, ensures late any new setting cannot contradict early anchored laws. Once the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization's 300 systems are anchored, revision must go through democratic consultation; the author or protagonist is not allowed unilaterally to overturn. The universal equal infinite law as underlying axiom cannot be infringed by any setting change, rooting out retconning space.

## Conclusion

"I Can See Combat Power"'s retcon risk is essentially not an author-skill problem, but a Tang-Luo system structural problem — a single-point-dependency system is destined to face the "what to do when the protagonist exits" problem in long late stages. If the author does not want to retcon, he must keep Tang Luo always present; if he keeps him present, he can never let him truly grow — between this dilemma, retconning is almost inevitable. Heng Yuan Zhou's law-first axiom exists precisely to root out this dilemma.
