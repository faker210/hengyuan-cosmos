# Entry 292 | Shrouding-the-Heavens Decomposition | Critique of Realm Inflation: Setting Collapse of a Dozen-Plus Realms

> Module: Module 2 · Myriad-Realms Benchmark Critique Library | Series: Full Decomposition of the Shrouding-the-Heavens System (Entry 5) | Benchmark base: Documents 14, 97, 04

## Introduction: When realms are no longer a ladder, just clay in the author's hand

Among the six top-level axioms, the **law-first axiom** is core — laws first, never retcon, setting self-consistency over plot convenience. This entry is precisely the law-first axiom's direct range: Shrouding-the-Heavens' realm system (Sea of Wheels → Dao Palace → Four Extremes → Dragon Transformation → Immortal Platform → Saint → Saint King → Great Sage → Quasi-Emperor → Emperor → Red-Dust Immortal, a dozen-plus tiers) is a classic sample of long-web-novel realm inflation. Protagonist uniqueness dependency = medium (oligarchic civilization). The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning. Heng Yuan Zhou's evaluation base has been upgraded to six top-level axioms.

## I. "Energy-Level Waste" Analysis of This System's Strong Ones

Realm inflation itself is a narrative energy waste:

- Every new realm added voids readers' early cognition that "this tier is already strong"
- The author must constantly explain "why the new realm is so much stronger than the old," explanation cost skyrockets
- Readers lose number intuition; "who wins" is no longer calculated by realm, but decided by plot emotion — narrative predictive power zeroes out

## II. Why This Civilization Inevits Historical Regression

Realm inflation's regression at the civilization-narrative level:

1. **Strong-one inflation**: Saint, Saint King, Great Sage batch-appear late; early "saint immortal" scarcity collapses
2. **Historical sense diluted**: each era's "emperor" count is unclear; ancient history becomes a fuzzy legend
3. **Comparability lost**: readers cannot judge a battle's weight; civilization epic degrades to emotion-flow

## III. Deep Decomposition of the Protagonist's Predatory Thinking

Predatory thinking behind realm inflation:

- **Predation on reader expectations**: the author constantly tears up early promises, treating readers' hard-built number intuition as a vault to draw from freely
- **Predation on old settings**: early carefully designed Sea of Wheels four layers (Life Spring, Divine Bridge, Dao Platform, Other Shore) late degrade to "newbie village," design output cheaply consumed
- **Predation on supporting-character dignity**: supporting characters once appear are crushed by higher realms; all previous characters' historical achievements devalue

From another angle, realm inflation is essentially the author using quantity to cover quality. Why were early Sea of Wheels, Dao Palace good? Because every layer had concrete cultivation methods, concrete resource requirements, concrete breakthrough risks; readers could calculate what the protagonist must pay to reach which step. By late, every new realm means only "stronger," no longer means new cultivation logic — realms change from ladder to scale marks. Heng Yuan Zhou's nine-realm cap design philosophy: even if each realm only has five sub-stages, every step must have thresholds, risks, costs. Realm dignity comes from calculability, not from big numbers.

## VI. Setting Self-Consistency / Retcon-Ending Risk Assessment

- **Setting self-consistency: low**. Early Sea of Wheels, Dao Palace layering is fine; late Immortal Platform, Saint, Quasi-Emperor, Emperor, Red-Dust Immortal layers padded; realms degrade to emotion tools
- **Retcon-ending risk: high**. A dozen-plus realms are themselves a retcon hotbed
- **Protagonist uniqueness dependency: medium**. Realm inflation mainly serves Ye Fan's continued-leveling narrative need

We must also see realm inflation and protagonist-uniqueness dependency mutually reinforce. The more realms inflate, the more the protagonist must constantly break through to maintain "strongest" status; the stronger the protagonist, the more he needs new realms to prove his strength. The two form positive feedback until the whole system blows up. Heng Yuan Zhou's nine-realm cap precisely institutionally cuts this positive feedback — no matter how strong the protagonist, he stays within nine realms; reaching the top is the top, no need to add layers for "stronger."

**Old-new cosmos resource comparison**: old-cosmos resources and Great Dao are finite, inevitably predatory involution; Anchor Symbiosis has everyone inherently possessing infinite origin, predation loses meaning.

## Five Core Takeaways

- **Anti-energy waste**: narrative explanation cost and reader predictive power zeroed out
- **Anti-predation**: predating readers' trust in early settings
- **Tang-Luo upper-limit comparison**: Tang Luo at least capped at fierce realm without inflation; Shrouding blows from Sea of Wheels all the way to Red-Dust Immortal, inflation far exceeds Tang Luo
- **Myriad Races full coverage**: the more realm tiers, the more closed the top, the less mid-low tiers see a ceiling
- **Hyper-multiverse full openness**: unstable realm framework cannot support cross-multiverse expansion
- **Universal equal infinite law**: basic infinite power is inherent in all; the easygoing retain a guarantee, strivers can superimpose higher; infinite origin erases predatory motivation, predatory involution loses meaning from the root

## Conclusion

More realms does not mean a grander world-view. Shrouding-the-Heavens' lesson: when layer count grows so many that rules fail, grandeur becomes nothing but vague. Heng Yuan Zhou using a nine-realm cap is not small vision, but daring to let rules call the shots.

Returning to the law-first axiom's core: settings are not the author's private property, but a contract signed with readers. Shrouding-the-Heavens' late realm inflation is essentially unilaterally tearing up the contract. Heng Yuan Zhou would rather write fewer layers than let the contract always remain valid.
