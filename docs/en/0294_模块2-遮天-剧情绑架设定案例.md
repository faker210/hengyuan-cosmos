## Introduction: Rules are meant to be broken — but only when the protagonist needs

Among the six top-level axioms, the **law-first axiom** expressly states "plot must not kidnap setting." This entry uses Shrouding-the-Heavens' concrete cases to dissect the operating mechanism of "plot kidnaps setting," a chronic long-web-novel disease. Laws can be expanded, but expansion must go through the "legislative procedure," not the "protagonist's rage" procedure. Protagonist uniqueness dependency = medium (oligarchic civilization). The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning. Heng Yuan Zhou's evaluation base has been upgraded to six top-level axioms.

## I. "Energy-Level Waste" Analysis of This System's Strong Ones

Energy waste caused by plot kidnapping:

- To let the protagonist "just barely win," villain strength is temporarily adjusted; narrative tension distorts
- Readers learn "anyway the protagonist will turn it around"; suspense mechanism fails, emotional investment wasted
- Supporting characters forcibly dumbed down or fed XP to衬托 the protagonist; supporting-character narrative energy wasted in vain

## II. Why This Civilization Inevits Historical Regression

Regression plot kidnapping creates inside the world-view:

1. **Strong-one ranking arbitrary**: who is strong or weak depends not on cultivation logic, but on who this scene needs to win
2. **Rule authority collapses**: the world's physical laws (cultivation system) are proven modifiable at will; this world no longer feels "real"
3. **Historical weight imbalanced**: important supporting characters' sacrifices only serve protagonist emotion; civilization history degrades to the protagonist's backdrop

## III. Deep Decomposition of the Protagonist's Predatory Thinking

Plot kidnaps setting is essentially predation on setting-authority:

- **Protagonist immunity**: Ye Fan always breaks through, comprehends, lucks out at critical moments; this is "protagonist-exclusive setting-modification authority"
- **Supporting characters instrumentalized**: supporting characters' meaning is to feed the protagonist XP, intelligence, emotion; their cultivation logic is cut at will
- **Enemies staircased**: enemy strength always one notch above / one notch below the protagonist, only to let the protagonist "just barely win"; the world becomes a training ground revolving around the protagonist

Plot kidnapping setting also has a hidden consequence: it makes the world lose "self-running" ability. A healthy world-view should be: even if the protagonist is absent some day, the world still runs by rules; supporting characters still have their own reasons to act. But Shrouding-the-Heavens' world, once Ye Fan does not participate, supporting characters' behavioral logic immediately blurs — they do not know what to do, only wait for Ye Fan to push. This shows Shrouding-the-Heavens' world was never an independently running world, but a stage revolving around the protagonist. Heng Yuan Zhou's Myriad Races republican design is precisely to let every supporting character, every race have their own goals, interests, action logic — the protagonist is one member, not the world's engine.

## VI. Setting Self-Consistency / Retcon-Ending Risk Assessment

- **Setting self-consistency: low**. The protagonist's critical-moment comprehension, lucking-out, cross-tier frequency is too high
- **Retcon-ending risk: high**. Plot kidnapping is retconning's direct precursor
- **Protagonist uniqueness dependency: medium**. The kidnapping's beneficiaries are precisely Ye Fan and his peer oligarchs

Plot kidnapping setting also produces a special kind of reader fatigue: readers no longer look forward to "how the protagonist wins by wisdom and rules," but to "how the protagonist turns it around this time." When the turnaround itself becomes expectation, victory loses weight. Why Shrouding-the-Heavens' late battles feel "empty" is precisely because readers know Ye Fan will definitely win, definitely have a way; so the battle process is no longer suspense, just procedure. Heng Yuan Zhou requires the protagonist to win within the law framework — victory is calculable, costly, truly requires applying rules — not by the author temporarily modifying rules.

**Old-new cosmos resource comparison**: old-cosmos resources and Great Dao are finite, inevitably predatory involution; Anchor Symbiosis has everyone inherently possessing infinite origin, predation loses meaning.

## Five Core Takeaways

- **Anti-energy waste**: suspense fails, supporting characters instrumentalized, emotion distorted
- **Anti-predation**: predating setting-modification authority, predating supporting characters' fates
- **Tang-Luo upper-limit comparison**: the Tang-Luo world's protagonist leveling also has a halo, but not to the "rules step aside at any time" degree
- **Myriad Races full coverage**: supporting characters, foreign races, ancients are all plot tools, no independent personality
- **Hyper-multiverse full openness**: a kidnapped world-view cannot serve as a cross-plane baseline
- **Universal equal infinite law**: basic infinite power is inherent in all; the easygoing retain a guarantee, strivers can superimpose higher; infinite origin erases predatory motivation, predatory involution loses meaning from the root

## Conclusion

"Changing rules for the thrill" is the cheapest thrill. The true hard-core is: rules are there, the protagonist still wins — because he obeys and skillfully uses rules, not because he makes rules kneel to him.

Plot kidnapping setting's final cost is making the author lose reverence for his own world. When the author feels rules can serve himself, he no longer respects this world; and an author who does not respect his own world cannot make readers respect this world.
