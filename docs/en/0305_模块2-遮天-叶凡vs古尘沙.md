# Entry 305 | Shrouding-the-Heavens Decomposition | Ye Fan vs. Gu Chensha: Oligarchic vs. Single-Log Comparison

> Module: Module 2 · Myriad-Realms Benchmark Critique Library | Series: Full Decomposition of the Shrouding-the-Heavens System (Entry 18) | Benchmark base: Documents 30, 17, 01

## Introduction: Between two ceilings lies a whole civilization's distance

Among the six top-level axioms, the republican axiom of Myriad Races opposes tying civilization's survival to one person; on the five-tier protagonist-uniqueness-dependence scale, Gu Chensha = extreme / single-log type, Ye Fan = medium / oligarchic type. This entry uses Dragon Talisman's Gu Chensha as contrast to locate Ye Fan's position in the Myriad-Realms protagonist genealogy. The law-first axiom reminds: comparison is not about combat power, but about civilization structure. Protagonist uniqueness dependency = medium (oligarchic civilization). The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning. Heng Yuan Zhou's evaluation base has been upgraded to six top-level axioms.

## I. "Energy-Level Waste" Analysis of This System's Strong Ones

Two civilizations' energy-waste forms differ:

- **Gu Chensha (single-log)**: first from antiquity, casually creates worlds, all beings "are made dragons"; energy all tied to him alone; the stronger he gets, the more idle all beings are
- **Ye Fan (oligarchic)**: depends on a strong-one group (Ye Fan + peer emperors + trusted line), energy dispersed in a small circle, still has some institutional prototype

Comparison conclusion: Ye Fan's energy waste is slightly lighter — he at least is not the sole pillar, but outside the small circle is still idle.

## II. Why This Civilization Inevits Historical Regression

Single-log and oligarchic regression mechanisms:

1. **Gu Chensha**: if he disappears, all beings have no self-rescue ability; regression probability approaches one
2. **Ye Fan**: if the oligarchic group exits as a whole, civilization still regresses, but with slightly more buffer — changing people within the group is slightly more possible than replacing Gu Chensha
3. Both share: neither writes order into institutions; both rely on "strong ones watching"

## III. Deep Decomposition of the Protagonist's Predatory Thinking

Predation form comparison:

| Dimension | Gu Chensha (single-log) | Ye Fan (oligarchic) |
|-----------|------------------------|---------------------|
| All beings' position | Objects being made dragons | The protected |
| Transmission way | Antiquity's first pities | Trusted line selects heirs |
| Governance power | All to Gu Chensha | Shared within oligarchic group |
| Myriad Ranks subjectivity | Fully outsourced | Slight participation but minimal |

Ye Fan's predation is more "dispersed," but essentially still takes civilization's fate from Myriad Ranks' hands.

Single-log and oligarchic also have a key difference: tolerance for "exit." If Gu Chensha disappears, the world instantly loses its engine; if Ye Fan and his circle disappear, the world will be chaotic for a while, but residual order can barely hold. This difference looks small, actually decides two civilizations' historical resilience. Oligarchic civilization is like a team propped by a few starters; when starters are injured it can still barely play a few games; single-log civilization is a team with only one player; he leaves the field and it completely stalls. Heng Yuan Zhou's networked civilization requires every position to have qualified backups — anyone exits, the match goes on.

## IV. Comparison With Anchor Symbiosis's Complete Alternative

| Dimension | Single-Log (Gu Chensha) | Oligarchic (Ye Fan) | Network (Heng Yuan Zhou) |
|-----------|--------------------------|---------------------|--------------------------|
| Dependence | Extreme | Medium | Extremely low |
| Civilization form | Single-log | Oligarchic | Networked |
| Order basis | One person | Small circle | Institution + network |
| Exit risk | Civilization collapses | Turmoil | Unaffected |

Ye Fan is "slightly better" than Gu Chensha, but both are intrinsically distant from Heng Yuan Zhou's networked civilization.

## V. Quest Sediment Domain Simulation Review Plan

1. **Modeling**: replicate two civilizations' power concentration
2. **Variables**: remove "Gu Chensha" and "Ye Fan and his group" separately, observe collapse speed
3. **Observation**: civilization recovery time, Myriad Ranks' self-rescue ability
4. **Review**: conclusion — oligarchic collapses slightly slower than single-log, but still far slower than network's "does not collapse"

## VI. Setting Self-Consistency / Retcon-Ending Risk Assessment

- **Setting self-consistency**: Gu Chensha system (Dragon Talisman) high self-consistency (casual world creation but internal rules clear); Ye Fan system medium-low self-consistency
- **Retcon-ending risk**: Gu Chensha low risk; Ye Fan high risk
- **Protagonist uniqueness dependency**: Gu Chensha = extreme; Ye Fan = medium

Gu Chensha and Ye Fan also have an interesting contrast: Gu Chensha "bears sins for all beings," Ye Fan "suppresses chaos for all beings." Different form, same structure — both outsource responsibility that should be jointly borne by Myriad Ranks to one (or a group of) strong ones. Myriad Ranks in both stories are passive receivers. What Heng Yuan Zhou wants to break is precisely this "outsourcing mentality": sins must be faced by Myriad Ranks themselves, turmoil must be governed by Myriad Ranks themselves; cannot be outsourced to any sage. The more outsourced, the less Myriad Ranks can self-rescue.

**Old-new cosmos resource comparison**: old-cosmos resources and Great Dao are finite, inevitably predatory involution; Anchor Symbiosis has everyone inherently possessing infinite origin, predation loses meaning.

## Five Core Takeaways

- **Anti-energy waste**: single-log wastes on all beings being idle, oligarchic wastes outside the small circle
- **Anti-predation**: both take civilization's fate from Myriad Ranks' hands
- **Tang-Luo upper-limit comparison**: Tang Luo = high dependence / bestowed type, between Gu Chensha and Ye Fan
- **Myriad Races full coverage**: neither single-log nor oligarchic achieves Myriad-Ranks co-governance
- **Hyper-multiverse full openness**: only networked civilization can stably expand cross-multiverse

Gu Chensha and Ye Fan also share a blind spot: both assume "all beings need someone stronger." Gu Chensha bears sins for all beings, Ye Fan suppresses chaos for all beings, but neither asks whether all beings want to be treated this way. Heng Yuan Zhou's fundamental stance: all beings do not need to be done-for; what they need is ability and opportunity. Giving one person greater power to protect Myriad Ranks is inferior to giving Myriad Ranks enough ability to protect themselves.
- **Universal equal infinite law**: basic infinite power is inherent in all; the easygoing retain a guarantee, strivers can superimpose higher; infinite origin erases predatory motivation, predatory involution loses meaning from the root

## Conclusion

Ye Fan is "more like a normal civilization" than Gu Chensha — because he still has peers, a faction, faction politics. But this is only picking the taller among dwarves: from "one person saves the cosmos" to "a group saves the cosmos," what Heng Yuan Zhou wants is "the cosmos saves itself."

Gu Chensha and Ye Fan, one is a single log, the other a few pillars; what Heng Yuan Zhou wants is the entire forest. A single log and pillars both look better than a forest, but only a forest remains a forest when any one tree falls.
