# Entry 368 | Dou Continent · Retcon-Ending Deep Analysis: Late Setting Overturning Early

> Module: Module 2 · Myriad-Realms Benchmark Critique Library | Series: Dou Continent System Decomposition (Entry 9) | Benchmark base: Documents 97, 89, 09

## Introduction: Rules set early, personally torn up late

Dou Continent is a model negative textbook of the law-first axiom. Early iron laws — century rings already scarce, Soul Master breakthrough must hunt, Title Douluo rare under heaven — were the pillars supporting the whole book's credibility. Late, to let Tang San keep climbing, these pillars are pulled out one by one: hundred-thousand-year rings mass-produced, spirit souls kill-free, Extreme Douluo everywhere, divine ranks layered. Readers are not "accepting an upgrade," but "forgiving a retcon." This entry, taking the law-first axiom as core, does a deep dissection of retcon-ending. Protagonist uniqueness dependency: **high (gift-style leaning oligarchic)**. The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning. Heng Yuan Zhou's judging base has been upgraded to the six top-level axioms.

## I. "Energy-Level Waste" Analysis of This System's Strong Ones

- **Early effort voided**: the hard-won breakthroughs the protagonist struggled for early are outdone late by casually obtained stronger resources; effort's narrative value hollowed out
- **Setting-credit loss**: every overturned early iron law subtracts a point of readers' trust in the worldview
- **Foreshadowing abandoned**: rule suspense planted early cannot be retrieved late due to setting changes

## II. Why This Civilization Inevits Historical Regression

Retcon-ending's blow to "civilizational credibility" is structural:

1. **Rules fickle**: when rules can be overturned by plot, all beings cannot plan their fate by rules; civilization expectation collapses
2. **Experience devalued**: previous generations' cultivation experience voided by rule changes; knowledge cannot accumulate
3. **Authority arbitrary**: the relation between strong ones and rules becomes "strong ones say change, it changes"; Myriad Ranks lose a law to rely on

## III. Deep Decomposition of the Protagonist's Predatory Thinking

- **Rules for my use**: wherever Tang San walks, rules make way there — he does not climb within rules, but makes rules change around him
- **Interpretation monopoly**: ultimately only Tang San and his Divine Realm have the right to announce "the rules are actually like this"; all beings can only accept
- **Destruction = leveling**: every retcon is accompanied by a protagonist leap; the benefit of rule-breaking is enjoyed solely by the protagonist

## VI. Setting Self-Consistency / Retcon-Ending Risk Assessment

- **Setting self-consistency: low**. Multiple early iron laws overturned late; early and late cannot coexist
- **Retcon-ending risk: extreme (this series' #1 negative textbook)**. Sequel crossover further stacks contradictions
- **Protagonist uniqueness dependency: high**

Retcon-ending's root is not the author's pen power, but law not preceding. Early-laid iron laws, once they become plot stumbling blocks, are casually kicked aside. Dou's problem is: its iron laws were never constants from the start, but functions of the protagonist's height — the protagonist grows an inch, rules retreat a foot.

Retcon-ending is also horrifying because it is "contagious." Once the author discovers "retcons can solve plot problems," they use it repeatedly, forming path dependence. Early Dou still barely kept rules; late treats retcon as a regular tool. Setting sinks from "must obey" to "changeable anytime"; the trust contract between author and readers breaks — this hurts the worldview more than any single contradiction.

Retcon-ending in Dou also has a concrete manifestation: the scarcity of early "century rings scarce" was built by the protagonist's group hunting through near-death. Readers tense up with them precisely because this thing is hard to get. Late hundred-thousand-year rings and spirit souls are casually obtainable; that year's tension becomes a joke. Setting credit is thus worn away bit by bit by "giving too easily." Heng Yuan Zhou preserves scarcity via law precedence — good things are good things, not casually handed to whoever plot needs.

**Old-new cosmos resource comparison**: old-cosmos resources and Great Dao are finite, inevitably predatory involution; Anchor Symbiosis has everyone inherently possessing infinite origin, predation loses meaning.
