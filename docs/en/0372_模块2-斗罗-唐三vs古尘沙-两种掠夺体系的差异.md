# Entry 372 | Tang San vs. Gu Chensha: Differences Between Two Predatory Systems

> Module: Module 2 · Myriad-Realms Benchmark Critique Library | Series: Dou Continent System Decomposition (Entry 13 · Comparison) | Benchmark base: Documents 251, 04, 17

## Introduction: One grabs stock, one creates increment

Gu Chensha (Dragon Talisman) and Tang San are two extremes of web-literature predatory systems: Gu Chensha is known for the single-log apex of "bearing sins for all beings, casually creating universes"; Tang San for systematic predation of "hunting soul beasts, deifying the whole family." Their protagonist-uniqueness dependency differs — Gu Chensha is **extreme (single-log)**, Tang San is **high (gift-style leaning oligarchic)**. This entry, taking the law-first axiom as core, compares the texture difference between the two predatory systems. Protagonist uniqueness dependency: **Tang San = high (gift-style leaning oligarchic)**. The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning. Heng Yuan Zhou's judging base has been upgraded to the six top-level axioms.

## I. "Energy-Level Waste" Analysis of This System's Strong Ones

- **Gu-Chensha-style waste**: energy immense but all beings "made dragons by him"; everything credited to the single greatest person; civilization's self-organization ability atrophies
- **Tang-San-style waste**: energy limited but institutionalized; predation spread into every cultivator's daily routine; larger in scale, more hidden
- **Difference**: Gu Chensha is "one person too strong so waste"; Tang San is "one system too bad so waste"

## II. Why This Civilization Inevits Historical Regression

1. **Gu-Chensha path**: single-log — protagonist falls, all beings have no support
2. **Tang-San path**: systemic — even if Tang San steps down, the soul-ring-hunting system still runs; regression is structural, not personal
3. **Conclusion**: Tang-San-style regression is harder to fix than Gu-Chensha-style — because it grows in institutions

## III. Deep Decomposition of the Protagonist's Predatory Thinking

- **Gu Chensha**: predation in "exclusive occupation of the apex" — he admits all beings can become dragons, but the achievement depends entirely on his mercy
- **Tang San**: predation in "killing to seize Origin" — he shouts no grand slogans, yet writes predation into every level
- **Hardness comparison**: Tang San's predation is harder (irreversible killing); Gu Chensha's predation is softer (warm outer garment); but in structural damage, Tang San's institutional predation covers more ground

## IV. Comparison With Anchor Symbiosis's Complete Alternative

| Dimension | Gu Chensha Single-Log | Tang San Systemic | Anchor Symbiosis |
|-----------|------------------------|-------------------|------------------|
| Dependency | Extreme | High | Extremely low |
| Predation form | Exclusive apex | Institutional killing | Develop increment |
| Repair difficulty | Medium (topple one person) | High (must change institutions) | No repair needed |
| Way out | Together become dragons | Path D man-made Dao-Law rings | Myriad Ranks republicanism |

The alternative's crux: for Tang San, what needs changing is not the protagonist, but the institution itself.

For Tang-San-style systemic predation, Heng Yuan Zhou's antidote is Path D · human-engineered Dao-Law soul rings and Lin Hao-style reconstruction: not hoping for a kinder protagonist, but directly changing institutions — rewriting "every ring must be seized" into "Dao laws grow by themselves." Lin Hao, as a victim of old rules, proves institutional predation is not unchangeable, as long as someone is willing to turn from victim into rule-rewriter.

## VI. Setting Self-Consistency / Retcon-Ending Risk Assessment

- **Setting self-consistency: low (Tang San)**: Gu Chensha's worldview also late inflates, but Tang San retcons more frequently
- **Retcon-ending risk: extreme (Tang San)**
- **Protagonist uniqueness dependency**: Tang San = high; Gu Chensha = extreme

The comparison between Tang San and Gu Chensha reveals the fate of two kinds of predation: Gu Chensha's single log, topple one person and it may be solved; Tang San's system, even with a new protagonist, still runs the same. The former's lesion is in the person, the latter's lesion is in the law — hence Heng Yuan Zhou's diagnosis of Tang San is "must change the operating system," not "change to a better protagonist."

Tang-San-style system and Gu-Chensha-style single log also have a key difference: Gu Chensha at least admits all beings "made dragons by him"; his grand narrative still has all beings' shadow; Tang San's system compresses all beings into hunt targets and backdrops. The former's problem is "bearing on behalf of all beings"; the latter's problem is "consuming all beings to feed." Heng Yuan Zhou criticizes the latter more heavily, because it did not even reach the step of "seeing all beings."

Tang-San-style system and Gu-Chensha-style single log can also be compared on "all-being subjectivity": Gu Chensha bears sins for all beings, at least admits all beings are "those on whose behalf sins are borne"; in Tang San's system, soul beasts do not even qualify for "sins borne on their behalf"; they are merely consumed objects. Heng Yuan Zhou's demand of Tang San is therefore higher: not only must predation stop, but the consumed must be re-included as "members with subjectivity." Gu Chensha's problem is overstepping his mandate; Tang San's problem is seeing as nothing — the latter is farther from Myriad Ranks republicanism.

Supplementary verdict: Tang-San-style system is harder to treat than Gu-Chensha-style single log, because the lesion is in the law, not the person. Gu Chensha topple one person and it may be solved; Tang San with a new protagonist still hunts. This is also why Heng Yuan Zhou strikes Tang San harder — what it requires is not a kinder protagonist, but an operating-system change. Lin Hao-style reconstructors' value lies precisely in that he touches the law, not the person.

**Old-new cosmos resource comparison**: old-cosmos resources and Great Dao are finite, inevitably predatory involution; Anchor Symbiosis has everyone inherently possessing infinite origin, predation loses meaning.

## Five Core Takeaways

- **Anti-energy waste**: one is waste from being too strong, one is waste from institutions
- **Anti-predation**: Tang San makes predation everyday and institutional
- **Tang-Luo upper-limit comparison**: Tang San is harder than Tang Luo, because Tang Luo at least did not make predation a universal procedure
- **Myriad Races full coverage**: Tang San's institutional predation harms the widest swathe of Myriad Ranks
- **Hyper-multiverse full openness**: both predations are extremely far from a symbiotic cosmos
- **Universal equal infinite law**: basic infinite power is inherent in all; the easygoing retain a guarantee, strivers can superimpose higher; infinite origin erases predatory motivation, predatory involution loses meaning from the root

## Conclusion

Gu Chensha's problem is "one person bears too much"; Tang San's problem is "a bad system makes everyone grab." The former topple one person and it may be solved; the latter must be pulled up by the root — this is precisely what Path D and Lin Hao do.
