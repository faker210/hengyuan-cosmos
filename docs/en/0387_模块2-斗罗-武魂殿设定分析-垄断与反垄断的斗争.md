# Entry 387 | Dou Continent · Spirit Hall Setting Analysis: The Struggle Between Monopoly and Anti-Monopoly

> Module: Module 2 · Myriad-Realms Benchmark Critique Library | Series: Dou Continent System Decomposition (Entry 28 · Situation Analysis) | Benchmark base: Documents 47, 36, 19

## Introduction: A monopolist overthrown, what was gained in return

Spirit Hall is the Dou world's greatest villainous organization — it monopolizes Spirit-Master resources, oppresses commoners, and aims to dominate all under heaven. Tang San overthrows Spirit Hall, read as justice defeating evil. But what Heng Yuan Zhou asks is not "should Spirit Hall fall," but "after it falls, did monopoly really disappear?" The answer is disappointing: Tang San overthrew Spirit Hall's monopoly, but pocketed monopoly into his own family. This entry, taking the law-first axiom as core, dissects this cycle of monopoly and anti-monopoly. Protagonist uniqueness dependency: **high (gift-style leaning oligarchic)**. The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning. Heng Yuan Zhou's judging base has been upgraded to the six top-level axioms.

## I. "Energy-Level Waste" Analysis of This System's Strong Ones

- **Spirit Hall monopoly**: concentrates resources, but uses them to maintain hegemony
- **Tang San anti-monopoly**: after overthrowing hegemony, resources are not universal-benefited, but transfer into Tang San's line
- **Waste cycle**: overthrowing the monopolist ≠ eliminating monopoly; only changing the monopolist

## II. Why This Civilization Inevits Historical Regression

1. **Monopoly not removed**: Spirit Hall falls; Clear Sky Clan and Divine-Realm family rise
2. **Struggle cost**: the anti-monopoly war consumes large energy; post-war no institution-building
3. **Commoners unchanged**: overthrowing Spirit Hall, commoners' cultivation qualification has no substantive improvement

## III. Deep Decomposition of the Protagonist's Predatory Thinking

- **Anti-monopoly cloak**: Tang San raises arms in the name of "anti-hegemony," morally impregnable
- **But does not establish anti-monopoly institutions**: he overthrew a monopolistic organization, but did not write "anti-monopoly" into law
- **New monopolist**: in the end Tang San and his family became the new, higher-tier monopolist

## IV. Comparison With Anchor Symbiosis's Complete Alternative

| Dou Monopoly Cycle | Anchor-Symbiosis Alternative |
|--------------------|-------------------------------|
| Spirit Hall monopoly | Anti-monopoly written into constitution |
| Tang San overthrows Spirit Hall | Anti-monopoly institutions, not changing protagonist |
| New monopolist Tang family | Knowledge and resources publicized |
| War solves monopoly | Institutions root-cure monopoly |

The alternative's crux: anti-monopoly relies not on overthrowing one person, but on writing anti-monopoly into the charter.

The real way to anti-monopoly is publicizing core technologies like Path D · human-engineered Dao-Law soul rings, rather than setting up a new monopolist. Lin Hao's choice to make technology public after overthrowing old rules is precisely to avoid the cycle of "overthrow Spirit Hall, gain the Tang family." Anti-monopoly constitutionally written, knowledge publicized, then monopoly cannot be done no matter who comes to power.

## VI. Setting Self-Consistency / Retcon-Ending Risk Assessment

- **Self-consistency: medium-low**. Spirit Hall's scale and ability stretch several times early-late
- **Retcon-ending risk: medium-high**
- **Protagonist uniqueness dependency: high**

The real victory of anti-monopoly is not beating Spirit Hall, but beating "monopoly" itself. Tang San won the war but did not establish anti-monopoly law, so monopoly continued under a new name. Heng Yuan Zhou's answer is plainer: publicize core technology, write anti-monopoly into the charter, make it impossible no matter who comes to power.

The Spirit Hall anti-monopoly struggle also has a lesson: after overthrowing the monopolist, if rules are not rewritten, the new entrant soon copies the old monopoly. Tang San overthrew Spirit Hall, but tilted resources and divine positions toward his own home; essentially monopoly's blood transfusion, not its end. Heng Yuan Zhou's constitutionally-written anti-monopoly wants, no matter who comes to power, monopoly to be impossible — solidify the struggle's fruit from "change persons" to "change law."

The Spirit Hall struggle also reveals a common anti-monopoly trap: people easily equate "overthrowing the monopolist" with "eliminating monopoly." Tang San achieved the former, not the latter. What Heng Yuan Zhou's constitutionally-written anti-monopoly must make up is precisely this lesson — monopoly is a structure, not some person. As long as the structure remains, overthrow the old monopolist, and a new one grows in the same place. The real victory is making that position unable to grow monopolists ever again.
