# Entry 388 | Dou Continent | Shrek Academy: Elite Education and Class Hardening

> Module: Module 2 · Myriad-Realms Benchmark Critique Library | Series: Dou Continent System Decomposition (Entry 29 · Situation Analysis) | Benchmark base: Documents 37, 43, 36

## Introduction: The academy that only takes monsters — whom did it raise

Shrek Academy is known for "only taking monsters, not ordinary people," the symbol of Dou-world elite education. It raised Tang San and his top strong companions, yet also raised education's threshold extremely high — those lacking talent do not even qualify for education. Heng Yuan Zhou does not ask whether Shrek is good or bad, but whom it shut out. This entry, taking the law-first axiom as core, dissects the relation between this elite academy and class hardening. Protagonist uniqueness dependency: **high (gift-style leaning oligarchic)**. The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning. Heng Yuan Zhou's judging base has been upgraded to the six top-level axioms.

## I. "Energy-Level Waste" Analysis of This System's Strong Ones

- **Selection threshold**: only top-talent accepted; large numbers of "mediocre but hardworking" people are turned away
- **Elite island**: academy resources concentrated on a tiny few geniuses; commoner education blank
- **Demonstration misplacement**: Shrek held as education model, yet models "education only for geniuses"

## II. Why This Civilization Inevits Historical Regression

1. **Class reproduction**: the elite academy generation after generation only takes elite children; poor families rarely produce high achievers
2. **Knowledge stratification**: top knowledge concentrated in the academy; commoners cannot access it
3. **Ceiling hardened**: academy graduation = above-people; education becomes a class-reproduction tool

## III. Deep Decomposition of the Protagonist's Predatory Thinking

- **Elite network**: Tang San's circle forms the strongest alumni network at Shrek; resources and connections concentrate into this circle
- **Threshold exclusion**: "only taking monsters" is itself an exclusionary predation — clutching education qualification in the hands of a few
- **No expansion**: after Shrek became famous, it did not push commoner education reform

## IV. Comparison With Anchor Symbiosis's Complete Alternative

| Shrek Elite Education | Anchor-Symbiosis Alternative |
|-----------------------|-------------------------------|
| Only takes monsters | Education Guarantee Law; everyone has school |
| Threshold exclusion | Symbiotic education system; multiple paths |
| Elite island | Cross-plane mentor network universal-benefited |
| Class reproduction | Social structure and class mobility constitutionally written |

The alternative's crux: education should not be geniuses' club, but all beings' staircase.

The antidote to elite education is universal benefit; Path D · human-engineered Dao-Law soul rings is precisely the key to universal benefit: it does not look at martial-soul talent, letting hardworkers turned away by Shrek also have a road. What Lin Hao represents is not "more genius monsters," but "a system that lets ordinary children also rise." Education should be all beings' staircase, not geniuses' club.

## VI. Setting Self-Consistency / Retcon-Ending Risk Assessment

- **Self-consistency: medium**. Academy setting largely self-consistent
- **Retcon-ending risk: medium**
- **Protagonist uniqueness dependency: high**

Shrek's problem is not whether it teaches well, but that it only teaches "monsters." When education shuts out those lacking talent, it becomes a class-reproducer rather than an upward channel. A truly good academy should let the most ordinary child also rise, not reserve the staircase only for geniuses.

Shrek's elite education also exposes a selection paradox: it only recruits people who are already geniuses, yet never bears the responsibility of "making ordinary people strong." Thus the more successful it is, the more it proves talent theory; the more it proves talent theory, the more it shuts ordinary people's doors. A truly great academy lies not in raising a few monsters, but in letting originally mediocre people also shine — education's meaning is amplifying effort, not screening bloodline.

Shrek's elite education also implies a resource mismatch: the best teachers and resources concentrate on serving geniuses who need it least, while ordinary people who need help most get nothing. This is like giving all medicines free to healthy people, yet letting patients queue to die. Heng Yuan Zhou's symbiotic education system pursues marginal-utility maximization — resources invested where they most change fate, not where they most manufacture legends. Education's fairness lies precisely here.
