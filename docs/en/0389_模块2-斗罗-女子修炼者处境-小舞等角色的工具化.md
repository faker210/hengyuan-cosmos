> Module: Module 2 · Myriad-Realms Benchmark Critique Library | Series: Dou Continent System Decomposition (Entry 30 · Situation Analysis) | Benchmark base: Documents 51, 26, 08

## Introduction: The strongest heroine may also be the most needed tool

The republican axiom of Myriad Races includes that human men and women alike have roads. Dou-world female cultivators, on the surface, do not lack strong ones — Xiao Wu and Bibi Dong both count as top. But what Heng Yuan Zhou asks is: their strength serves themselves, or serves the protagonist's growth and plot? Xiao Wu sacrifices herself for Tang San, is resurrected, and finally becomes his Divine Empress — her strength always revolves around the protagonist. This entry, taking the law-first axiom as core, dissects this "surface strong, actually instrumentalized" situation. Protagonist uniqueness dependency: **high (gift-style leaning oligarchic)**. The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning. Heng Yuan Zhou's judging base has been upgraded to the six top-level axioms.

## I. "Energy-Level Waste" Analysis of This System's Strong Ones

- **Xiao Wu's sacrifice**: her cultivation and life are used by the narrative as fuel for Tang San's leveling and emotional elevation
- **Female strong ones' subordinate position**: even as strong as Bibi Dong, arranged as Tang San's opposite and stepping stone
- **Energy serves plot**: female characters' strength curve stretches with the protagonist's needs

## II. Why This Civilization Inevits Historical Regression

1. **Female subjectivity absent**: female cultivators' fates are decided by the male protagonist's plot
2. **Path attachment**: female strong ones' endings are mostly bound to "becoming someone's Dao-companion / Divine Empress"
3. **Distorted demonstration**: this tells readers, however strong a woman, she cannot escape serving the male protagonist

## III. Deep Decomposition of the Protagonist's Predatory Thinking

- **Sacrificial extraction**: Xiao Wu's sacrifice is sung as love, but structurally the protagonist plundered her life as growth resource
- **Supporting-character subjectivity stripped**: female characters have no complete growth line of their own
- **Bibi Dong instrumentalized**: her complexity is simplified into "the villain the protagonist must defeat"

## IV. Comparison With Anchor Symbiosis's Complete Alternative

| Dou Female Instrumentalization | Anchor-Symbiosis Alternative |
|--------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Sacrifice to fulfill the protagonist | Female cultivators' paths independent, subjectivity complete |

The alternative's crux: women should not be fuel for the protagonist's leveling; she should be the protagonist of her own story.

To let female cultivators truly independent, they must not have to sacrifice to fulfill the protagonist. Path D · human-engineered Dao-Law soul rings provide growth fuel not attached to a male protagonist; Lin Hao's reconstruction path also emphasizes complete subjectivity. Xiao Wu need not again sacrifice her life for Tang San; a female strong one's ending should be chosen by herself, not arranged by plot as Divine Empress.

## VI. Setting Self-Consistency / Retcon-Ending Risk Assessment

- **Self-consistency: medium-low**. Female characters' strength stretches with plot
- **Retcon-ending risk: medium-high**
- **Protagonist uniqueness dependency: high**

Xiao Wu's sacrifice is sung as love, yet few ask: did she have an option not to sacrifice? When a female strong one's fate is decided by the male protagonist's plot, however strong she is only an exquisite tool. What Heng Yuan Zhou wants is for every step of a female cultivator to be walked for herself.

Female instrumentalization also shows in the emotional line's asymmetry: Xiao Wu sacrificing for Tang San is the narrative's climax; Tang San giving for Xiao Wu is more "repayment" than "equal sacrifice." This structure makes female characters' emotions always first extracted, later repaid. What Heng Yuan Zhou wants is not women stronger, but that their giving need not be premised on "needed by the protagonist" first — their fate should be defined by themselves.

Female instrumentalization also connects to the abuse of "sacrifice narrative." Dou repeatedly uses female characters' sacrifice to elevate the male protagonist's realm — sacrifice, immolation, fulfillment, almost became female characters' standard function. Once this narrative mode solidifies, readers default that "women's greatness lies in dedicating to the protagonist." What Heng Yuan Zhou must break is precisely this narrative inertia: female characters' greatness can lie in her own growth, her own choices, rather than in how much she gives to the protagonist.
