Manghuang's sixteen realms inflated to collapse; Heng Yuan Zhou's nine-plus-two stably expands. The difference is not "dare to be big," but "legislate first or follow plot to change first." This entry directly compares the two approaches. Protagonist uniqueness dependency: Manghuang = high-leaning-medium, Heng Yuan Zhou = extremely low. The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning. Heng Yuan Zhou's judging base has been upgraded to the six top-level axioms.

Energy waste when law does not precede: every time Manghuang inflates, early setting must be overturned and retold; both author's and reader's trust consumed. - **Manghuang retcon-ending risk: high**
- **Heng Yuan Zhou retcon-ending risk: extremely low**

Law precedence also means one thing: the author cannot stand above his own setting. Manghuang's biggest problem is not many realms, but the author can change anytime. Heng Yuan Zhou writes law above everyone — including the author; new dimensions can only join by appendix caliber. This looks like chaining the story, but is actually insuring the worldview. Rules the author can overturn anytime are, to readers, equal to no rules; only rules even the author cannot casually change deserve to be called law.

Law precedence also means setting is not the author's private property. Manghuang treats setting as play-dough; Heng Yuan Zhou treats setting as constitution. Play-dough can be kneaded freely; amending the constitution goes through procedure. This seemingly rigid procedure is precisely why the worldview survives.
