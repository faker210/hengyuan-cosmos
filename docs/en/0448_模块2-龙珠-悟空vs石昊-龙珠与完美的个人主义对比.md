# Entry 448 | Dragon Ball · Goku vs. Shi Hao: Individualism Comparison of Dragon Ball and Perfect World

> Module: Module 2 · Myriad-Realms Benchmark Critique Library | Series: Dragon Ball Full Decomposition (Entry 17 · Cross-Protagonist Comparison 4) | Benchmark base: Documents 17, 04, 20

## Introduction: two people who "decree eternity alone"

Shi Hao (*Perfect World*) is single-log civilization's apex: he walks out of Stone Village, fights all the way above Sacrificial Dao, in old age "decrees eternity alone," buries darkness along with himself into the river of time. Goku is gift-style-leaning-oligarchic representative: he does not "decree eternity alone," but Earth's survival hangs on him alone. Both are individualism's peak — one uses "bury me" to carry everything, one uses "I fight" to carry everything. This entry, by the law-first axiom and anti-predation axiom, compares these two individualisms' structural differences.

This entry's judging base is the six top-level axioms and five mandatory cores, with the law-first axiom as core. The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning.

## I. "Energy-Level Waste" Analysis of This System's Strong Ones

- **Goku's waste**: Zeno-tier aptitude for personal combat; civilization zero institutional spillover
- **Shi Hao's waste**: Sacrificial-Dao-tier aptitude for "decreeing eternity alone" — carrying all darkness, all enemies, all causality himself; all beings protected so well they "do not need to grow their own abilities"
- **Comparison**: Shi Hao at least "died for all beings"; Goku did not even die; but the two's common lesion: **all beings are protected so well they lose the chance to stand up themselves**

## II. Why This Civilization Inevits Historical Regression

- **Goku's world**: strong one absent = collapse; civilization has no institutions
- **Shi Hao's world**: after Shi Hao buries himself into the river of time, all beings live, but "decreeing eternity alone" means all crises were carried by him alone — later generations never experience true life-or-death tests; is civilization truly mature?
- **Common lesion**: neither designed "let civilization itself experience crises, grow itself"

## III. Deep Decomposition of the Protagonist's Predatory Thinking

- **Goku's predation**: innocent type — enemies as EXP packs
- **Shi Hao's predation**: sacrificial type — he "decrees eternity alone" looks most selfless, actually plunders all beings' chance to face darkness themselves, grow themselves, bear it themselves
- **Comparison**: Goku's predation hidden in combat instinct; Shi Hao's predation hidden in sacrifice — the latter most concealed, because he buried even himself

## Five Core Takeaways

- **Anti-energy waste**: both's energy not truly institutionalized; Goku for fighting, Shi Hao for decreeing eternity
- **Anti-predation**: Goku plunders enemies as leveling fuel; Shi Hao plunders all beings' growth opportunity
- **Tang-Luo upper-limit comparison**: Tang Luo's "universal benefit" is closer to republic than Shi Hao's "decree alone," but neither truly reaches Myriad-Ranks co-build
- **Myriad Races full coverage**: both worlds' Myriad Ranks are "protected/decreeed-over" objects, not co-build subjects
- **Hyper-multiverse full openness**: individualism civilization cannot truly expand, because everything hangs on one person
- **Universal equal infinite law**: basic infinite power inherent in all; easygoing retain guarantee, strivers can superimpose higher; infinite origin eliminates predation motive

## Conclusion

Goku and Shi Hao are individualism's two faces: one laughs and wins the world, one buries himself and buries eternity. Both are great, but both made the same mistake — they let all beings believe "someone carrying for us is enough." Heng Yuan Zhou's answer: let Myriad Ranks stand up themselves, not because strong ones do not sacrifice, but because standing-up Myriad Ranks walk farther than decreeed-over Myriad Ranks.

One more layer: Shi Hao's "decreeing eternity alone" is in readers' eyes the greatest sacrifice — he alone buried darkness into the river of time. But Heng Yuan Zhou wants to ask: did he bury darkness, or all beings' ability to face darkness? If Myriad Ranks never truly experienced darkness, never overcame darkness themselves, do they deserve the words "mature civilization"? Goku at least let Earthlings experience Saiyan invasion, Cell Game, Buu crisis (though revived every time); Shi Hao carried all darkness himself, later generations do not even have "having suffered" experience. From civilization-growth angle, Shi Hao's sacrifice actually makes all beings more childish — this is the paradox of "protection."
