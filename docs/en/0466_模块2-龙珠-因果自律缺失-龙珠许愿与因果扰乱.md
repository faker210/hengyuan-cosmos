# Entry 466 | Dragon Ball · Causal Self-Discipline Absence: Dragon-Ball Wishing and Causal Disruption

> Module: Module 2 · Myriad-Realms Benchmark Critique Library | Series: Dragon Ball Full Decomposition (Entry 35 · Civilization Analysis 8) | Benchmark base: Documents 19, 03, 49

## Introduction: when revival needs no price

The causal self-discipline axiom requires: cross-plane operations strictly filed, observe first then limited interference, only return knowledge without disturbing causality. Dragon Ball world is precisely this axiom's complete opposite — Dragon-Ball wishing turns "revival," "restoration," "reality rewriting" into zero-threshold operations. The dead can revive, cities can restore, time can reverse; Frieza can revive, Cell can revive, Buu can revive. This entry takes the **causal self-discipline axiom** as core, deeply analyzing Dragon Ball world's causal disruption.

This entry's judging base is the six top-level axioms and five mandatory cores, with the law-first axiom as core. The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning.

## I. "Energy-Level Waste" Analysis of This System's Strong Ones

Causal disruption's energy waste:

- **Revival = history zeroed**: every wish to revive the dead erases civilization's trauma memory; history does not advance
- **Causal chain broken**: after the dead revive, their original causality (funeral, mourning, inheritance) all erased
- **Wish = shortcut**: whoever gathers Dragon Balls takes the biggest cosmos-tier resource — this resource does not rely on cultivation, contribution, legislation

## II. Why This Civilization Inevits Historical Regression

How causal disruption feeds historical regression:

1. **Law of causality hollowed out**: because the dead can revive, "death" is no longer the end; civilization's reverence for life diluted
2. **Effort loses meaning**: since can wish revival, why cherish life? Since can wish restore, why build cities?
3. **History discontinuous**: because setting changes anytime, revival rules added with plot; civilization has no true "history"

## III. Deep Decomposition of the Protagonist's Predatory Thinking

Goku's role in causal disruption:

- **Accepting wishing no threshold**: Goku's party repeatedly gathers Dragon Balls to "revive companions"; never reflects "how much causality this revival disrupted"
- **Revival = solving problems**: after fighting Buu, after fighting Zamasu, after fighting Jiren, it always ends "wish restore everything" — Goku never truly solves problems; just erases problems by wish
- **No reverence for causality**: Goku never treats the dragon god as "a causal being to revere," but as "a tool person to use when he wants"

- **Causal setting self-consistency: low** (revival rules change with plot: one wish a year → three wishes → cross-universe wishes → Black-Star Dragon Balls planet explodes after wish)
- **Causal self-consistency expected: high** (revival goes approval, trauma memory retained, Alaya trail-marked)

## Five Core Takeaways

- **Anti-energy waste**: revival zeroes history, invalidates effort, erases trauma memory
- **Anti-predation**: public miracle (dragon god) privately diverted as wishing tool
- **Tang-Luo upper-limit comparison**: Tang Luo at least raised cultivation by effort; Goku solves problems by wishing
- **Myriad Races full coverage**: among Myriad Ranks only those who gather Dragon Balls can use the dragon god
- **Hyper-multiverse full openness**: world with collapsed law of causality cannot truly expand into stable multiverse
- **Universal equal infinite law**: basic infinite power inherent in all; easygoing retain guarantee, strivers can superimpose higher; infinite origin eliminates predation motive

## Conclusion

The Dragon Ball wishing mechanism is Dragon Ball worldview's most charming and most dangerous design — it realized childhood wishes, also hollowed out civilization's law of causality. Heng Yuan Zhou's answer is the causal self-discipline axiom: any reality-rewriting operation must be filed, approved, trail-marked; revival is not forbidden, but must go legal process, must retain trauma memory, must let Myriad-Ranks parliament know. Let the dragon god return to "Myriad Ranks' public miracle" position; this is Dragon Ball causal disruption's true way out.

One more layer: Dragon Ball wishing mechanism's deepest harm is not "reviving people" itself, but that it makes "death" lose weight. When readers know "anyway can revive," when characters know "anyway can revive," when writers know "anyway can revive" — death is no longer narrative's ultimate seriousness, but only "temporary exit." Gohan dies in Cell Game? Revive. Buu kills all Earthlings? Revive. Frieza kills Namekians? Revive. Over forty years, Dragon Ball world's death scenes can no longer truly make readers tense — because everyone knows, the last Dragon Ball will solve everything. This is causal-self-discipline absence's corrosion on narrative seriousness, and also on civilizational reverence.
