# Entry 481 | Jackie Chan Adventures · Predatory-Thinking Decomposition: Talismans-as-Resources Zero-Sum Game

> Module: Module 2 · Myriad-Realms Benchmark Critique Library | Series: Jackie Chan Adventures Decomposition (Entry 4) | Benchmark base: Documents 04, 06, 26

## Introduction: good guys and bad guys, the same zero-sum chessboard

Heng Yuan Zhou (Myriad-Spirits Balanced-Anchor Origin Universe)'s six top-level axioms — anti-predation axiom, republican axiom of Myriad Races, collective ascension axiom, causal self-discipline axiom, **law-first axiom**, universal equal infinite law as core — judges "predatory thinking" not by protagonist's good/evil, but by protagonist's bottom assumption about resources. The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning. Jackie and Valmont, Shendu are opposed in good/evil, but on the assumption "talismans are zero-sum resources" they are completely aligned.

## I. "Energy-Level Waste" Analysis of This System's Strong Ones

The zero-sum game itself is the biggest energy waste: both sides invest massive adversarial cost for the same resource; these costs net zero to civilization. Good guys win, resources move from vault A to vault B; bad guys win, move back. Across hundreds of episodes, no one truly "created" anything.

## II. Why This Civilization Inevits Historical Regression

Under zero-sum structure, both sides in the game are shaped by the other: Jackie's side to "win" must become as secret, as hard, as unscrupulous as the opponent. Across hundreds of episodes, Section 13 from an "agency protecting cultural relics" became a "secret organization snatching treasure with the mafia." **Zero-sum games also train good guys into predators.**

## III. Deep Decomposition of the Protagonist's Predatory Thinking

Jackie's predatory thinking has three layers:

1. **Resource-private assumption**: talismans must be "held" by one side, cannot be public.
2. **Our-side-first assumption**: Jackie's side holding talismans is "safer," so our taking is right.
3. **Adversarial investment rationalized**: to prevent bad guys from getting it, good guys must also invest massive resources to "grab."

These three assumptions stacked, Jackie becomes a "good-guy-version predator" — his difference from bad guys is only that he locks grabbed things in a safer warehouse, not uses them to dominate.

## IV. Comparison With Anchor Symbiosis's Complete Alternative

| Zero-Sum Game | Anchor-Symbiosis Alternative |
|---------------|-------------------------------|
| Talismans ours / theirs | Talismans belong to all humanity |
| Good guys and bad guys grab | Talisman principles public; no "grabbing" |
| Secret agency operates | Public legislation, public research |
| Adversarial investment | Develop increment; do not consume stock |

## V. Quest Sediment Domain Simulation Review Plan

Quest Sediment Domain injects variable "when Jackie gets the first talisman, immediately holds a global press conference to publicize its principles." Simulation shows: within three episodes, villains lose reason to exist (because talismans no longer secret); within ten episodes, the scientific community begins replicating talisman power; after thirty episodes, the "talisman contest" plotline disappears entirely. **The zero-sum game's collapse only needs one person choosing not to play.**

This simulation also reveals a hidden feature of zero-sum games: it is not broken by "the stronger side," but broken by "the side that refuses to play." Jackie does not need to be stronger than Valmont; he only needs to publicize talisman principles — the zero-sum game loses its soil. This has implications for all predatory IPs: **breaking predation does not require stronger force; it requires the choice to refuse predation.** What Heng Yuan Zhou needs to do is institutionalize this choice — make "public" the default option, not the hero's occasional good deed.

Jackie vs. Valmont comparison, on the surface is good/evil comparison, actually is two players on the same chessboard. Jackie locks talismans in Section 13; Valmont locks talismans in his vault — both are "possessing talismans"; difference only where they lock. Heng Yuan Zhou asks: **why must talismans be "locked"?** Why cannot they be "public"? This question Jackie and Valmont both cannot answer — because both default talismans are zero-sum resources. Only by jumping out of this default can talismans turn from "lock" into "bridge."

Jackie Chan Adventures' zero-sum thinking also manifests in its definition of "who is a good guy." Good guy = grabs talismans and locks them well; bad guy = grabs talismans and misuses them. The whole animation defaults "talismans must be grabbed by one side"; never appears the third position of "talismans should not be grabbed." Heng Yuan Zhou's position is precisely this third: **talismans should not be grabbed; talismans should be studied, replicated, popularized.** Once this position appears, the good-guy/bad-guy dichotomy fails — because "grabbing" itself is negated.

Heng Yuan Zhou's position is not "good guys should grab talismans," but "talismans should not be grabbed." These two positions seem similar, actually fundamentally different — the former still on the zero-sum chessboard; the latter directly flips the chessboard. **The one who flips the chessboard is the true breakthrough maker.**

## VI. Setting Self-Consistency / Retcon-Ending Risk Assessment

- **Setting self-consistency: medium**. Zero-sum game self-consistent within children's animation framework, but not self-consistent under real civilizational logic
- **Retcon-ending risk: medium**
- **Protagonist uniqueness dependency: medium-high**

**Old-new cosmos resource comparison**: old-cosmos resources and Great Dao are finite, inevitably predatory involution; Anchor Symbiosis has everyone inherently possessing infinite origin, predation loses meaning.

## Five Core Takeaways

- **Anti-energy waste**: adversarial investment net contribution zero
- **Anti-predation**: good and bad guys on the same zero-sum chessboard
- **Tang-Luo upper-limit comparison**: Tang Luo at least broke aristocratic monopoly; Jackie defaults "talismans can only be grabbed"
- **Myriad Races full coverage**: talisman contest only between Earth's few factions
- **Hyper-multiverse full openness**: zero-sum thinking locked civilization's imagination
- **Universal equal infinite law**: basic infinite power inherent in all; easygoing retain guarantee, strivers can superimpose higher; infinite origin eliminates predation motive

## Conclusion

Jackie is a good person. But a good person standing on a zero-sum chessboard, playing the same game as bad guys, no matter how by-the-book, is still a predator. What Heng Yuan Zhou wants is not "a good guy who wins the game," but **the one who flips the chessboard and returns resources to everyone.**
