# Entry 483 | Jackie Chan Adventures · Setting Self-Consistency and Retcon-Risk Assessment

> Module: Module 2 · Myriad-Realms Benchmark Critique Library | Series: Jackie Chan Adventures Decomposition (Entry 6) | Benchmark base: Documents 04, 14, 19

## Introduction: giving a childhood animation a "cosmos checkup"

Heng Yuan Zhou (Myriad-Spirits Balanced-Anchor Origin Universe)'s six top-level axioms — anti-predation axiom, republican axiom of Myriad Races, collective ascension axiom, causal self-discipline axiom, **law-first axiom**, universal equal infinite law as core — scores every benchmarked IP on "setting self-consistency / retcon-ending risk." The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning. This entry scores *Jackie Chan Adventures* overall.

## I. "Energy-Level Waste" Analysis of This System's Strong Ones

Jackie Chan Adventures as a long animation, its setting waste mainly in "ability-boundary drift": talisman power weak/strong, with serving plot as first criterion. This itself is narrative-energy waste — early-established "talisman rules" repeatedly broken; audience trust repeatedly overdrawn.

## II. Why This Civilization Inevits Historical Regression

A children's animation can tolerate setting drift, but a civilization cannot. If talisman power is weak/strong, rules change at will, then any talisman-based civilizational planning is unreliable — **rules unstable, civilization cannot sediment.**

## III. Deep Decomposition of the Protagonist's Predatory Thinking

Jackie as protagonist, his "always wins" less and less from kung fu itself, more and more from "plot needs him to win." This victory does not reward effort, only rewards protagonist identity.

## V. Quest Sediment Domain Simulation Review Plan

Quest Sediment Domain makes *Jackie Chan Adventures* a "season-by-season self-consistency snapshot": Season 1 self-consistency ≈ medium-high; after Season 2 ≈ medium; later seasons ≈ medium-low. Talisman ability boundaries, demon settings, mask rules occasionally contradict across seasons. Overall, its retcon risk within children's animation range is medium — because it never intended to build a serious cosmos from the start.

Specifically, *Jackie Chan Adventures*'s self-consistency leakage has two nodes: first node at "talisman ability boundary drift" — early Horse talisman can only heal wounds, late becomes can eliminate all side effects; second node at "demon and mask settings stacking" — each season introduces new villains, old villains' ability settings simplified or forgotten. These two nodes stacked, early-established "talisman rules" repeatedly stretched. But compared to Douluo, Dragon Ball, Dragon Rune, Jackie Chan Adventures' retcon degree is lighter — it positioned from the start as children's adventure animation; readers' expectation of it is not "serious cosmos," but "fun adventure." This positioning protected it from strict scrutiny.

Heng Yuan Zhou scores *Jackie Chan Adventures* self-consistency "medium," because it is still reasonably self-consistent within children's animation range — talisman ability boundaries though drift, drift range not large; demon and mask settings though stack, not like Dragon Rune that "casually creates universes." Its retcon risk is also "medium" not "high." But Heng Yuan Zhou's judgment does not lower standards because it is a children's animation — **children's animation should also have stable rules, because children learn "how the world works" through animation.** If children see "rules change with plot," they will learn "rules can be not obeyed." This is retcon's deepest harm: it is not just the author's problem; it is transmitting to readers a "rules are untrustworthy" worldview.

Heng Yuan Zhou's final judgment on *Jackie Chan Adventures*: it is an excellent children's adventure animation, but its worldview lacks the rule stability a serious cosmos should have. This instability can be excused within children's animation range, but in Heng Yuan Zhou's benchmark critique it must be recorded — because it represents a universal narrative tendency: **for plot fun, rules can yield.** This tendency not eradicated, long-form cosmoses can never establish true self-consistency.

Heng Yuan Zhou's final score on *Jackie Chan Adventures*: setting self-consistency medium, retcon-ending risk medium, protagonist uniqueness dependency medium-high. These three scores constitute its "checkup report" — acceptable within children's animation range, but under serious-cosmos standards still needs transformation. Transformation plan see Entry 484.

This is Heng Yuan Zhou's final checkup conclusion on Jackie Chan Adventures.
