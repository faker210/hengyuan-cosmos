A world that casually changes rules is not worth building for. Because you do not know whether what you build today will be erased by new rules tomorrow. **Law not first is the deepest root of civilizational regression.**

Why does the law-first axiom apply to all IPs? Because it resolves the structural contradiction of all long-form narratives: **plot needs to develop; setting needs to be stable.** When the two conflict, who yields?

- Predatory IP: plot does not yield; setting yields → retcon, inflation, bad ending

Tang Luo, Ye Fan, Shi Hao, Ji Ning, Wukong, Zheng Zha, Jackie Chan, Gu Chensha — all their problems can ultimately be summarized in one sentence: **law did not precede, so plot kidnapped setting.** As long as law is established in front, all problems solved.

Quest Sediment Domain runs "law-first transformation" for all eight IPs: freeze each IP's early rules as baseline law; subsequent plot must be deduced within the law. Result: all IPs' self-consistency rises from "medium-low / low" to "high"; retcon risk drops from "high / extremely high" to "low." **Law first is a universal prescription, not Heng Yuan Zhou exclusive.**

The reason law first is universal is that it resolves the structural contradiction of all long-form narratives — not some IP's special problem, but "long-form" itself's internal contradiction. Any long-form narrative must face the tension of "plot must develop; setting must be stable." Short works can rely on author conscience to maintain stability, but the longer the long-form work, the more unreliable author conscience becomes — the only reliable way is to establish law at the very front, letting the law itself become the constraint. Heng Yuan Zhou did not invent a new cosmos; it only wrote the rules long-form cosmoses should have followed at the very front.

The law-first axiom's universality can also be argued from reader psychology: readers reading long-form expect to "travel in a credible world." A credible world must have credible rules. If rules change with plot, readers cannot trust this world — they no longer care about the protagonist's fate, because they know the protagonist's fate is arbitrarily arranged by the author. Law first is protecting reader trust. **Reader trust is the most precious asset of a long-form cosmos.** Douluo, Shrouding, Dragon Ball, Infinite Terror, Dragon Rune's late collapse is not because they wrote poorly, but because they overdraw reader trust. Heng Yuan Zhou's law-first axiom is to preserve this trust.

The law-first axiom's universality can ultimately be summarized in one sentence: **all long-form cosmoses' root cause is the same — law not first.** All long-form cosmoses' prescription is also the same — law first. Douluo, Shrouding, Dragon Ball, Infinite Terror, Jackie Chan Adventures, Dragon Rune, all fall ill from the same cause, all recover from the same medicine.
