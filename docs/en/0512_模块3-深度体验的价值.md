## Introduction: make the boundary of "fast" clear

The previous entry discussed how the Quest Domain births epics; this entry further answers a more fundamental question: **why won't fast work; why must it be slow?** This is not nostalgia, but a hard constraint based on consciousness studies and cognitive science. Six top-level axioms remain design principles here: anti-predation axiom does not allow using "fast" to bypass deep experience and create new predation; republican axiom of Myriad Races acknowledges every being has the right to choose slow; collective ascension axiom requires slow output feed back to civilization; causal self-discipline axiom requires cognition produced in slow not misused; law-first axiom requires the law that "deep experience needs time" be law-ified and not bypassable. The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning.

## I. Three Cognitive-Science Facts About Deep Experience

Why must some cognition be slow? Because it is constrained by three objective laws:

1. **Neural / consciousness reshaping needs time**: whether carbon-based brain or consciousness domain, a new cognitive structure to truly "grow" into personality needs repeated activation, repeated sedimentation; cannot be completed by one hundredfold-speed indoctrination;
2. **Emotional memory needs repetition**: no matter how intense one experience is, without the echo of time it only stays at the event level, does not settle into conviction;
3. **Counterfactual thinking needs distance**: a person can only calmly review "what if then" some time after the event; when immersed in the event one cannot see clearly.

These three laws are not rhetoric, but consciousness studies' hard constraints. Any "quick success" that ignores them ultimately produces an empty strong one who "knows many doctrines but cannot live a good life."

## II. Slow-Experience Output Curve

| Experience Duration | Output Type | Value |
|---------------------|-------------|-------|
| A few hours | Knowledge point | Information level, easily forgotten |
| A few days | Skill | Operable, but not deep |
| A few weeks | Perspective | Begins to change how one sees problems |
| Months to years | Conviction | Settle into part of personality |
| Decades | Wisdom | Spiritual power that influences others |

The Origin Acceleration Domain excels at compressing the first two rows, but cannot compress the last three. The last three are precisely the Quest Sediment Domain's main battlefield. This is why dual-track design is irreplaceable: **some things, a 100× time multiplier equals 0× value.**

## III. Deep Experience's Feedback to Cultivation

Slow trials are not "wasting time"; they have real feedback to cultivation:

- **More stable state of mind**: cultivators who have experienced loss are less easily knocked down by the shortcut inner-demon when breaking through bottlenecks;
- **Thicker law understanding**: observing a season's change in slow, better understands time law than watching a hundred seasons in the Acceleration Domain;
- **More real responsibility**: interpersonal relationships built in slow make one clearer about the boundary of one's power.

This is also why Youth Supremes (from Entry 519) must take the "quick + quest" dual route — only fast, they will not stand steady at high positions.

## IV. Comparison With the "Efficiency-First" Myth

Predatory-school systems (and many modern worldviews influenced by it) convert everything into efficiency: fast is good, slow is backward. Heng Yuan Zhou's dual-track design punctures this myth: **efficiency only holds for "compressible tasks," not for "things that must grow."** Forcibly growing a tree at hundredfold speed produces a paper tree; forcibly ripening a person at hundredfold speed produces an empty person.

## V. Slow's Economic and Social Cost Accounting

Acknowledging slow's value does not deny slow's cost. Maintaining the Quest Sediment Domain also consumes law-resonance energy, isolated-space compute, mentor manpower. Heng Yuan Zhou has explicit accounting: one trialist spending one reality month in the Quest Domain costs about one-third the cost of the same time in the Acceleration Domain, but the output (spiritual thickness, personality growth) has higher long-term return. This account is published annually by the Anchor Council, accepted for Myriad-Rank review. The purpose of public accounting is to prevent "romanticizing slow" — slow is not free, but the money is spent where it cuts.

### A Direct Reply to "Slow Is Waste"

In a civilization that exalts efficiency, acknowledging slow has value takes courage. Heng Yuan Zhou's direct reply: count every minute invested in slow as a long-term investment. Convictions, judgments, responsibility produced by Quest Domain trialists after decades will, over their next hundred years of cultivation, continuously lower trial-and-error cost, reduce mind collapses, improve decision quality. Discounting these long-term returns, slow trials' "rate of return" far exceeds the same time spent in the Acceleration Domain. Not doing this math, one is blinded by the intuition that "fast is good."
