# Entry 529 | Myriad-Races Self-Chosen-Destiny Mechanism | Self-Chosen Destiny vs Predatory-School Fatalism

> Module: Module 3 · Dual-Track Time Domain | Benchmark base: Documents 04, 05, 40

## Introduction: is destiny fated, or chosen

Predatory-school systems' deep worldview is fatalism: some are born protagonists, some born cannon fodder, some races born slaves, some worlds born to be destroyed. This fatalism is not literary rhetoric; it is predatory legitimacy's philosophical basis — **since destiny was long ago fated, then the strong reaping the weak is "Heaven's Way."** Heng Yuan Zhou's Myriad-Races Self-Chosen-Destiny Mechanism, from the root, negates this worldview. Six top-level axioms are this showdown's highest framework: anti-predation axiom is the ethical basis for negating fatalism; republican axiom of Myriad Races is self-chosen destiny's source of legitimacy; collective ascension axiom is self-chosen destiny's direction; causal self-discipline axiom is self-chosen destiny's guarantee not to harm others; law-first axiom is the two destinies' criterion — which one better matches real laws. The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning.

## I. Contrast of Two Views of Destiny

| Dimension | Predatory Fatalism | Heng Yuan Zhou Self-Chosen Destiny |
|-----------|--------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Starting point | Born fated | All equal |
| Path | Decided by talent/bloodline/plot | Individual self-chosen |
| Strong and weak | Strength and weakness are Heaven's mandate | Strength and weakness are process |
| The weak's resistance | Violating Heaven's Way | Legitimate right |
| The world's endgame | The strong forever strong | Myriad Ranks ascend together |

## II. Why Fatalism Is Wrong

From the law-first axiom view, fatalism has two hard flaws:

1. **It does not match real laws**: in reality no one is born a protagonist; all species, all individuals are results of evolution;
2. **It cannot be self-consistent**: if destiny was long ago fated, then the strong predating the weak is "Heaven's Way," but this means Heaven's Way itself is encouraging entropy increase, ultimately inevitably leading to cosmos collapse — this contradicts real energy conservation and life-evolution laws.

Heng Yuan Zhou's self-chosen-destiny view is the reverse: it acknowledges difference (some high talent, some low talent), but denies destiny (differences can be rewritten through institutions, effort, mutual aid).

## III. Comparison With Tang Luo's "Protagonist Halo"

The Tang Luo system wrote fatalism to the extreme: the protagonist is not walking his own road; he is walking the road the author wrote; the stronger he gets, the more it proves "Heaven's mandate." Supporting roles' no matter how great their effort, only pave the road for the protagonist. This worldview's essence is disguising "authorial favoritism" as "Heaven's Way." Heng Yuan Zhou rips off this disguise: **no authorial favoritism, no Heaven's mandate, only the road Myriad Ranks themselves chose.**

## IV. Civilizational Transition From Fatalism to Self-Choice Theory

A civilization transitioning from fatalist worldview to self-choice worldview does not happen automatically. It requires institutions, education, technology all in place simultaneously: institutions writing the right to choose into the charter; education from primary school teaching "destiny is choosable"; technology using the dual-track time domain to let every being actually experience "I can choose." Heng Yuan Zhou in newly merged planes' transition period, will station Myriad-Rank representatives for decades, helping the civilization complete this transition. Transition is not forced, not rushed, but also not absent. Fatalism's shackle is not broken by one declaration, but slowly loosened only when a generation is truly treated as beings with the right to choose.

### Self-Chosen Destiny's Constraint on the Strong

The self-chosen-destiny mechanism not only protects the weak, but also constrains the strong. A strong one cannot because "I am stronger than you" choose destiny for the weak, nor because "the bridges I have crossed are more than the roads you have walked" decide direction for the young. The strong one's advantage lies in contribution, in guidance, in companionship, not in replacing. This constraint decouples "strong" from "dominate" — the strong may be stronger, but not more autocratic.

### Self-Chosen Destiny and the Source of "Meaning"

In a fatalist worldview, life's meaning comes from "being endowed" — endowed by gods, by bloodlines, by protagonist halos. In a self-chosen-destiny worldview, life's meaning comes from "being chosen" — you chose this path; this path therefore has meaning. The latter seems more tiring, because there is no ready-made meaning to inherit; but it is also more true, because meaning is grown by yourself, not stuffed to you by others. Heng Yuan Zhou chooses the latter, because it acknowledges every being is the author of his own meaning.

The showdown between fatalism and self-choice theory is ultimately not a philosophical showdown, but an engineering showdown: whoever can turn "I can choose" into a reality every being feels daily, wins. Heng Yuan Zhou bets on engineering, not slogans.

From fatalism to self-choice theory is not one revolution, but a road countless beings slowly walked in countless daily small choices. Institutions only pave this road; the walkers are still Myriad Ranks themselves.

## Conclusion

Destiny is not a pre-written script, but every being's choice made in every present moment. The Myriad-Races Self-Chosen-Destiny Mechanism is Heng Yuan Zhou's most fundamental response to predatory fatalism — it does not acknowledge anyone is qualified to write destiny for others.
