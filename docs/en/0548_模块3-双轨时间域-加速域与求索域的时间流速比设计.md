# Entry 548 | Dual-Track Time Domain | Design of the Time-Flow-Rate Ratio Between Acceleration and Quest Domains

> Module: Module 3 · Dual-Track Time Domain Civilizational-Experience System | Benchmark base: Documents 16, 502, 89

## Introduction: how much faster, how much slower is not a gut call, but an equation solved by law

The dual-track time domain's essence is not "there is fast and slow," but **the ratio between fast and slow**. The Origin Acceleration Domain tuned too fast hollows experience and overheats causality; the Quest Sediment Domain pressed too slow stalls civilization and misses rescue windows. How exactly to set this "ratio" is Module 3's first half's hardest engineering problem. This entry gives the time-flow-ratio design principle — it is not some genius's inspiration, but an equation constrained by six top-level axioms and repeatedly solved by sandboxes.

Six top-level axioms run through the design: anti-predation axiom requires both fast and slow tracks not rely on stealing time, but on efficiency amplification and incremental development; republican axiom of Myriad Races requires the ratio fairly adjustable for all beings; collective ascension axiom requires fast-track results and slow-track sedimentation feed each other proportionally; causal self-discipline axiom requires the two tracks' ratio not produce causal overheating and paradoxes; law-first axiom requires every gear of the ratio defined first by law, adjustable but not arbitrary. The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning.

## I. Why a "Ratio" Must Be Set, Not Independently Tuned

If the Acceleration Domain and Quest Domain each tune their own time, two problems arise:

1. **Output imbalance**: the Acceleration Domain produces a hundred technical proposals a day, the Quest Domain reviews only one a year; fast-track results pile up and rot at the slow track's door; fast was wasted.
2. **Personnel imbalance**: everyone wants to crowd into the faster Acceleration Domain; no one trials on the slow track; civilization loses thickness, returning to Dragon-Ball-style nihilistic reversal.

So a **flow ratio** must coordinate the two tracks' "output throughput" and "personnel allocation."

## II. Baseline Ratio and Three-Gear Design

The baseline design the sandbox solved is: **default Acceleration Domain 100×, Quest Domain near-real (about 0.9–1×), i.e., fast:slow ≈ 100:1.** On this baseline, three gears by task type:

| Gear | Fast:Slow | Typical Task | Design Rationale |
|------|-----------|--------------|------------------|
| R&D Gear | 100:1 | Technique deduction, new medicine synthesis | Fast track produces proposals; slow track stress-tests; throughput matched |
| Rescue Gear | 1000:1 | Sudden disaster, cross-plane emergency aid | Time is life; fast track extreme acceleration; slow track only as safety gate |
| Sediment Gear | 10:1 | Youth Supreme trial, epic review | Not too fast; guard experience thickness |

Three gears share one principle: **however many times the fast track is raised, the slow track's review capacity must be synchronously raised by the same factor**, otherwise the two tracks' closed loop breaks.

## III. Ratio and Experience Preservation

The ratio design's other hard constraint is **experience preservation**. The sandbox found: when an individual continuously stays on the fast track beyond the "fast duration" his consciousness can bear, on exit appears "time weightlessness" — feeling life has a hole. So the ratio design built-in cooldown rules:

- Fast-track continuous duration capped by individual consciousness capacity; at cap, forced cooldown;
- Cooldown period returns to near-real-flow Quest Domain, for digestion, review, emotional sedimentation;
- Alaya Network + multi-layer supervision monitors experience-preservation degree in real time; not up to standard, no re-entry.

This makes "fast" always bottomed by "slow," giving "acceleration ≠ hollow" ratio-level guarantee.

## IV. The Ratio's Law Anchoring and Dynamic Regulation

The ratio is not a welded constant, but a law-anchored adjustable parameter: the law layer defines "ratio adjustable range = 10:1 to 1000:1; crossing the boundary triggers causal alarm"; the actual value is dynamically computed and recommended by the Alaya Network per current civilizational throughput needs, causal load, personnel will, then approved by the Anchor Deliberative Council. No one may bypass approval to speed up privately — this is the causal self-discipline axiom's concretization in time engineering. Boundary-crossing speed-up is immediately circuit-broken by the Temporal-Paradox Prevention Mechanism (Document 89).

An often-overlooked supporting design is the "slow-track capacity" constraint: while raising the fast-track gear, must proportionally expand the Quest Sediment Domain's review and sedimentation capacity, otherwise fast-track output piles up and rots at the slow track's door. This constraint was once broken during an R&D sprint; of two thousand proposals deduced at 100×, nearly seven became void for lack of review. Henceforth "raise fast must match slow" was written as an iron law; no one dares starve the slow track for speed.

## V. Value Ranking Behind the Ratio

This time-ratio equation actually hides the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization's value ranking: it would rather R&D be a bit slower than let experience thin by a hair; it would rather civilization be a few years less fast than let causality crack by a hair. When someone demands "turn the ratio more aggressive," the law network does not look at who shouts loudest, only at this value ranking — fast is always the means; the thickness slow guards is the end. The ratio dispute on the surface is a number dispute; deep down is the dispute of "what civilization is fast for." Heng Yuan Zhou's answer is always: fast, is to let Myriad Ranks live with more dignity, not fast for fast's sake.

## Five Core Takeaways

- **Anti-energy waste**: gear fast and slow by task type; use "fast" only where fast is truly needed, avoiding the law-network overload of undifferentiated cosmos-wide acceleration
- **Anti-predation**: the ratio achieved by efficiency amplification and incremental development, not by stealing any being's time
- **Tang-Luo upper-limit comparison**: Tang Luo only the protagonist can enter the fast track; all beings can only slow-boil; Heng Yuan Zhou's fast and slow tracks open to Myriad Ranks proportionally, gear self-chosen
- **Myriad Races full coverage**: different lineages' beings have different consciousness capacities; ratio gears have adaptive suggestions for each ethnic group; 99% can enter the track
- **Hyper-multiverse full openness**: baseline ratio, three-gear design, cooldown rules, boundary-crossing circuit-break thresholds all public, for all civilizations to reference
- **Axiom 6 · Universal Equal Infinite Law**: all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will not be eliminated by the times; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity — infinite origin inherent in all; predation naturally loses its meaning

## Conclusion

The time-flow-ratio design turns "fast and slow" from a philosophical contradiction into a computable, calibratable, auditable engineering equation. The fast track wins speed for civilization; the slow track guards thickness for civilization; and that precise "ratio" is the invisible gear that keeps the two from swallowing each other, instead fulfilling each other. Heng Yuan Zhou dares let civilization accelerate a hundredfold relying not on guts, but on this law-anchored, sandbox-validated ratio.
