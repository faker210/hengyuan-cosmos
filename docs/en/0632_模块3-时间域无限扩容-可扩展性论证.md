# Entry 632 | Time Domain and Infinite Expansion | Scalability Argument of the Time-Domain System

> Module: Module 3 · Dual-Track Time Domain | Benchmark base: Documents 80, 78, 98 | Series: Time Domain and Law-First Axiom (Entry 2)

## Introduction: civilization will keep merging new planes — can the time domain hold up

One of law-first axiom's promises is "worldview may infinitely expand without collapse." For the dual-track time domain to live up to this promise, it must answer a forward-looking question: **as civilization keeps merging new planes and接入 new Myriad Ranks, can the time-domain system still hold up?** This entry argues the time-domain system's scalability — why it may expand from a small cosmos to cross-plane, hyper-multiverse, without collapsing.

Among the six top-level axioms, law-first axiom and republican axiom of Myriad Races are this entry's protagonists: scalability must be law-self-consistent (law-first), and must not leave any clan behind during expansion (Myriad-Race Republicanism); the other three — anti-predation, collective ascension, causal self-discipline — jointly constrain the expansion's way. The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning.

## I. Three Layers of Scalability

The time-domain system's scalability divides into three progressive layers:

1. **Intra-plane expansion**: within an already merged plane, continuously add acceleration-domain/Quest-domain nodes, capacity, gears.
2. **Cross-plane expansion**:接入 the time-domain interface to newly merged planes, letting new planes' beings also use the dual track.
3. **Scale expansion**: from material plane to energy layer, consciousness domain, law network; the time domain expands toward higher scales.

Three layers, magnified level by level, but none starts over — this is scalability's essence.

## II. Why It Can Expand: Three Design Pivots

The time domain's scalability rests on three design pivots:

| Pivot | Content | Expansion Meaning |
|-------|---------|-------------------|
| Interface Standardization | Alaya Network access protocol (Entry 614), switching protocol (Entry 612) are both public standards | New planes接入 by standard, no custom development |
| Resource Incrementalization | Energy by incremental development (Entry 613), the more used the more | Expansion does not cut the stock cake |
| Algorithm Publication | Allocation, conversion, load models public | New clans apply by their own parameters |

Three pivots mean: **expansion is "access," not "rebuild"**. This is scalability's engineering essence.

## III. Risks and Hedging in Expansion

Expansion is not risk-free. Main risks and hedging:

- **Risk 1: heterogeneous planes' time-unit differences**. Newly merged planes' time laws may differ from the origin plane. Hedge: time-unit conversion model (Entry 622) adapts by plane, does not assume unified time.
- **Risk 2: expansion too fast causing causal overheating**. Many new planes simultaneously接入, causal forks surge. Hedge: cross-plane exploration (Document 78) batches access, each batch does stress testing.
- **Risk 3: new clans' integration pain**. Newly merged Myriad Ranks unfamiliar with dual-track rules. Hedge: the Quest Domain provides "new-plane adaptation courses," helping them get familiar with rules.

## IV. Expansion and the Balanced Anchor Hyper-Multiverse Realm Interface

The time domain's final expansion direction is serving civilization's move toward the Balanced Anchor Hyper-Multiverse Realm (above Origin). Scalability argument therefore has long-term meaning:

- Only if the time-domain system may infinitely expand with civilization does civilization dare move toward cross-multiverse scale.
- Each expansion makes the dual-track time domain's interfaces, algorithms, institutions one notch more mature.
- The expansion process itself is the ongoing verification of law-first axiom's "never collapse."

In other words, **scalability is not "is it enough now," but "will it still hold when infinitely expanded in the future"**.

## Supplementary Argument: Only What Is Scalable Is a Living System

A system that can only serve one plane, one group of beings, no matter how exquisite, will eventually dry up with that plane's evolution. The dual-track time domain pursues scalability, essentially pursuing "alive" — it presets no endpoint, treats no plane as the last piece of the puzzle. Standardized interfaces let new planes plug-and-play; incremental energy makes it richer with expansion; public algorithms let clans apply as needed. This design philosophy contrasts with predatory-faction systems' dead end of "closed cosmos, expansion = collapse." Scalability is not a technical parameter, but a civilizational attitude: believing the future is bigger than the present, so every brick laid today must leave interfaces for tomorrow's building.
