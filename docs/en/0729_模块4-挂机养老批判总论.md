# Entry 729 | Critique of Hang-Up Retirement · Overview — The Common Malady of Web-Novel Heavenly Emperors and Immortal Sovereigns

> Module: Module 4 · Transcendence Orthodox Definition Library | Benchmark base: Documents 04, 17, 90

## Introduction: a common ending, a common malady

The last five entries of Module 4 will land critique on concrete IPs. But before critiquing Ye Fan, Shi Hao, Tang San, and Goku, we must first make clear the common traits of this "hang-up retirement" malady — why do all the Heavenly Emperors, Immortal Sovereigns, and cosmic overlords across the web-novel multiverse end up with the same disease? This is not one author's problem; this is the entire web-novel paradigm's structural problem.

Six top-level axioms simultaneously act as the critique's ruler in this entry: the anti-predation axiom points out these "Heavenly Emperors and Immortal Sovereigns"' power sources are generally unclean; the republican axiom of Myriad Races asks on what basis they monopolize the highest realm; the collective ascension axiom points out they do not carry Myriad Ranks; the causal self-discipline axiom points out their cross-plane operations are never reported; the law-first axiom points out their systems generally eat their own books and end in failure.

The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning.

## I. Four Symptoms of the Common Malady

Dissecting the endings of the four major IPs — Shrouding the Heavens, Perfect World, Douluo, Dragon Ball — the "Heavenly Emperor / Immortal Sovereign disease" has four common symptoms:

### 1.1 The Endpoint Is the Endpoint

After the protagonist reaches the summit, the story ends. He has no next step; he does not need one. His "transcendence" is a period, not a comma.

### 1.2 Myriad Ranks Stay Where They Are

After the protagonist ascends, the civilization behind him keeps running, but no longer progresses because of him. He is civilization's highest point, also civilization's ceiling — later generations only need to look up; no need to catch up.

### 1.3 Knowledge Privatized

The protagonist's manuals, experience, and law understanding are either passed only to bloodline disciples, or vanish with his ascension. No public sharing; no Selfless Dao.

### 1.4 Lonely Invincibility

The protagonist's final state is "invincible, immortal, lonely." He is not symbiotic with Myriad Ranks; he stands above Myriad Ranks. This loneliness is romanticized; readers take it as a strong one's medal.

Four symptoms together are "hang-up retirement" — **he has already hung up at the cosmos's highest point, producing no civilizational increment.**

## II. Why This Malady Is Structural

This malady is not one author writing off the rails; it is the web-novel paradigm's inevitable product:

- **爽文 logic**: readers want the rush of "the protagonist grows stronger," not the labor-sense of "the protagonist works." Once the author writes the protagonist invincible, the story's thrill point is exhausted;
- **Protagonist uniqueness**: the entire civilization revolves around the protagonist; the protagonist stops, the whole story stops. Protagonist-uniqueness-dependency ratings are generally "medium" to "extreme";
- **No post concept**: old web novels have no "transcended-one post" setting; strong ones have nothing to do after reaching the summit, so they can only hang up;
- **No Myriad-Rank perspective**: the story is told from the protagonist's view; Myriad Ranks are only backdrop. The author has no motivation to describe "what happened to Myriad Ranks after the protagonist reached the summit."

Four structural reasons together: "hang-up retirement" is not a bug; it is a feature — it is this paradigm's inevitable ending.

## III. The Three-Layer Harm of Hang-Up Retirement

Hang-up retirement looks harmless — the protagonist is already invincible; what's wrong with letting him rest? Actually it has three layers of harm:

### 3.1 Waste of Civilizational Increments

All of the protagonist's lifelong cultivation's computable power, law understanding, and civilizational influence stop producing at the moment he reaches the summit. This is cosmos-grade energy waste.

### 3.2 A Ceiling for Later Generations

The protagonist is too strong, too invincible, too lonely; later generations dare not chase, cannot catch up, need not chase. Civilization's evolution speed is locked by the protagonist personally.

### 3.3 Pollution of Readers' Values

After readers finish this kind of ending, they form a subconscious: **cultivating is for the day when you may not work.** This value seeping into reality is one of the cultural roots of "lying flat is justice." Heng Yuan Zhou Library's Module 4 opposes this value at the law level.

Heng Yuan Zhou's alternative to "Heavenly Emperor / Immortal Sovereign disease" is everything discussed in the previous 28 entries:

- Change "Myriad Ranks stay where they are" to "carry Myriad Ranks along" — the collective ascension axiom;
- Change "knowledge privatized" to "Selfless-Dao public library" — the anti-predation axiom;

## Conclusion

The Heavenly Emperor / Immortal Sovereign disease is not an individual author's problem; it is the entire web-novel paradigm's structural problem. Its symptoms are endpoint-is-endpoint, Myriad Ranks stay put, knowledge privatized, lonely invincibility. What Heng Yuan Zhou Library must do is tell all cultivators at the law level: **this is not a strong one's romance; this is a civilization's tragedy.**

Understand this malady's structural nature, and you understand why the next four entries' critique is not aimed at one character — Ye Fan, Shi Hao, Tang San, and Goku are all good characters; their tragedy is imposed on them by the paradigm. What Heng Yuan Zhou must do is not negate these characters, but give them (and the billions of readers behind them) a new option: **after growing strong, you need not hang up; after reaching the summit, you need not be lonely; after becoming invincible, you need not have no target.** The next four entries, one by one.
