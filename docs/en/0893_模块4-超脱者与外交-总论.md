# Entry 893 | Transcended Ones and Diplomacy · Overview — The Transcended One's Cross-Civilizational Diplomacy

> Module: Module 4 · Transcendence Orthodox Definition Library | Benchmark base: Documents 78, 894, 895, 896

## Introduction: the transcended one is not only a gatekeeper — he is also Myriad Ranks' diplomat

When Myriad-Rank republican territory expands from one plane to the multiverse, diplomacy is no longer an optional "icing on the cake," but survival necessity. The old world's strong ones have only two attitudes toward external civilizations: either closed-door isolation ("outsiders are all barbarians"), or strike out ("if you disobey, beat you until you obey"). Heng Yuan Zhou must find a third road: **establish relations with external civilizations through diplomacy — not subjugation, not conquest, but equal dialogue.** This entry is the diplomacy sub-theme overview.

Six top-level axioms simultaneously take the stage: the republican axiom of Myriad Races requires diplomacy be equal toward all civilizations; the anti-predation axiom requires diplomacy not target expansion; the collective ascension axiom requires diplomacy serve common evolution; the causal self-discipline axiom requires the diplomatic process be traceable; the law-first axiom requires diplomatic laws be self-consistent, not swayed by plot convenience; the universal equal infinite law confirms all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; easygoing layabouts retain a floor and are not eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes may superimpose higher infinity — infinite origin is inherent in all, predation naturally loses its meaning.

The universal equal infinite law: within the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization, all beings inherently possess basic infinite power; the easygoing retain a guaranteed basic infinity and will never be eliminated; strivers and Youth Supremes can superimpose more and higher levels of infinity. Infinite origin is inherent in all, so predation naturally loses its meaning.

## I. The Old World's Two Diplomatic Extremes

### Extreme 1: Closed-door isolation
"The outside world is dangerous; we shut the door and live our own lives." The problem is: the outside world will not stop coming because you shut the door. By the time it comes, you do not even know what it is.

### Extreme 2: Strike out
"We are strongest; beat all who disobey until they obey." The problem is: after beating them? Occupation costs exceed predation gains; occupying long enough, you become the new oppressor.

Both extremes' common problem is: **external civilizations are not treated as equal dialogue partners.** Either ignored, or conquered.

## II. Heng Yuan Zhou's Diplomatic Philosophy

Heng Yuan Zhou's cross-civilizational diplomacy is based on three principles:

1. **Equality**: whether the other civilization is stronger or weaker than us, diplomatic status is equal;
2. **Voluntariness**: no forcing any civilization to establish relations with us, nor accepting being forced;
3. **Reciprocity**: the purpose of establishing relations is mutual benefit, not unilateral extraction.

These three principles determine Heng Yuan Zhou's diplomacy is neither "heavenly court grace to distant peoples" nor "the strong demand the weak to submit" — it is truly equal dialogue.

## III. Four-Level Classification of Diplomatic Relations

Heng Yuan Zhou classifies external civilizations by closeness into four levels:

| Level | Relation | Content |
|-------|----------|---------|
| Level 1 | Friendly civilization | Full diplomatic relations, knowledge sharing, joint development, non-aggression |
| Level 2 | Neutral civilization | Limited exchange, non-aligned, non-hostile |
| Level 3 | Unknown civilization | Observe first, limited intervention; no proactive contact |
| Level 4 | Predatory-school civilization | No diplomatic relations, defense, self-defense and counterattack when necessary |

Diplomatic strategy adjusts with civilization level, but no discrimination — every civilization may go from Level 3 to Level 2 to Level 1.

## IV. The Transcended One's Role in Diplomacy

The transcended one bears the core role in cross-civilizational diplomacy:

- **Chief negotiator**: high-level diplomacy involving law frameworks and cross-plane agreements, the transcended one negotiates personally;
- **Technical translator**: different civilizations' law languages are completely different; the transcended one is responsible for translation and explanation;
- **Trust endorsement**: the transcended one's existence and Alaya records provide credibility for diplomatic agreements;
- **Conflict mediation**: when diplomatic relations are in crisis, the transcended one intervenes to mediate.

Cross-civilizational diplomacy's highest achievement is not how many agreements signed, but letting more and more civilizations discover: without fighting they may also live well. When symbiosis becomes an option, predation is no longer the only solution. This is diplomacy's power — it is cheaper than war, and more lasting than war.

Cross-civilizational diplomacy matters because in the multiverse most civilizations are neither predatory schools nor friendly civilizations — they are neutral, unknown, developing. Diplomacy's task is gradually transform these neutral civilizations into friendly ones, making predatory schools' isolation deeper and deeper.

Cross-civilizational diplomacy's deeper meaning is: it turns the "me vs. outside" opposition into the "us vs. other civilizations" network relation. When civilizations no longer see the outside world as threat, but as dialogue partner, the cosmos becomes safe.

Axiom 6's expression in the diplomatic field is: when all beings inherently possess basic infinite power, civilizations need not contest limited resources — every civilization may evolve infinitely on its own track. Diplomacy is not for dividing the cake, but for letting different civilizations on their own infinite tracks not interfere and inspire each other.

## V. Five Core Takeaways

- **Anti-energy waste**: diplomacy is far cheaper than war — use dialogue instead of confrontation
- **Anti-predation**: diplomacy does not target expansion, does not bully the weak
- **Tang-Luo upper-limit comparison**: Tang Luo either ignores or conquers external civilizations; Heng Yuan Zhou uses diplomacy to build a multi-civilizational network
- **Myriad Races full coverage**: diplomacy faces all civilization forms, including heterogeneous life
- **Hyper-multiverse full openness**: diplomatic agreement full texts and negotiation records (except sensitive) open network-wide through the Selfless Dao

## Conclusion

The transcended one's cross-civilizational diplomacy treats external civilizations as dialogue partners, neither enemies nor subjects. Equal, voluntary, reciprocal; four-level relation classification, the transcended one's core role, Alaya full-process records. Diplomacy is not weakness, but another form of security — more friends, fewer enemies. This is the transcended one's orthodox definition in the diplomatic dimension.

In the final analysis, the transcended-one-and-diplomacy overview described in this entry is no isolated case — its causal and energy-level traces are filed throughout in the Alaya Network, traceable and verifiable.
