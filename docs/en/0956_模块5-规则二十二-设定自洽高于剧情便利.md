# Entry 956 | Anti-Predation Heavenly-Dao Rules · Rule 22 | Setting Self-Consistency Above Plot Convenience

> Module: Module 5 · Civilization Final-Finalization Canonical Entries | Benchmark base: Document 20 | Series: Complete Collection of Anti-Predation Heavenly-Dao Rules

## Introduction: for the sake of being good, one may not be unreasonable

The old cosmos's cultivation stories have a common flaw: to make some segment "satisfying," they let the whole worldview "collapse." The protagonist must win, so the villain is temporarily made dumb; the heroine must be saved, so the treasure temporarily cheats; the realm gap is three major tiers, and one word of "sudden enlightenment" catches up. This is "plot convenience" overwhelming "setting self-consistency." Short-term it is satisfying; long-term the whole work's worldview credit collapses. Heavenly-Dao Rule 22 completely reverses this priority: setting self-consistency above plot convenience. Any narrative segment, any strong one's performance, must first pass the "is this possible under existing laws" test before being allowed to happen. Plot may be twisted, but may not cheat.

The six top-level axioms appear visibly here: the law-first axiom is this rule's body, directly writing "setting self-consistency above plot convenience"; the anti-predation axiom negates predation-style narrative that "to elevate the protagonist suppresses all supporting roles"; the republican axiom of Myriad Races decides in narrative Myriad Ranks are characters, not plot tools; the collective-ascension axiom requires plot serve whole-system ascension, not individual highlights; the causal self-discipline axiom requires each plot point's causal chain complete; Axiom 6 · Universal Equal Infinite Law supports from the base — since everyone inherently possesses basic infinite power, drama need not be manufactured by "protagonist-halo temporary cheats," plot tension may come from "whose infinity is superimposed higher," not "whom the author favors."

## I. Rule Content: Three Self-Consistency-First Checks

1. **First pass the law check**: before any event happens, first ask "does the current law library allow it?"; if not allowed, it does not happen;
2. **No temporary dumbing-down/cheating**: villains, supporting roles, and the protagonist's performance must match their established settings and realms; temporary adjustment for satisfaction points is forbidden;
3. **Causal chain complete**: an effect must have its cause; "suddenly won" and "suddenly understood" are forbidden.

If plot convenience conflicts with self-consistency, abandon convenience, keep self-consistency.

## II. Jurisprudential Basis

- **Causal-completeness argument**: the cosmic causal web does not allow "effect without cause"; forcibly manufacturing one manufactures causal cracks that eventually backlash;
- **Worldview-credit argument**: Myriad Ranks' trust in civilization rests on "settings are credible"; once an exception is made for a satisfaction point, credit is gone forever;
- **Infinite-origin argument**: under Axiom 6, drama need not be manufactured by favoritism, but may naturally arise from "the diversity of infinite superimposition" — the easygoing one's floor infinity and the striver's superimposed infinity are themselves the best plot tension.

## III. Execution Mechanism

- The Alaya Network does a "self-consistency precheck" for every major event: whether the event is consistent with the current law library, the parties' realms, and causal records;
- Those failing precheck are not allowed to "happen," returned to the narrator for redesign;
- A "self-consistency score" is set, scoring each civilizational project and each historical narrative; low scores are redone within a time limit;
- The Terminology Committee first calibrates settings prone to ambiguity, reducing "self-consistency disputes."

## IV. Consequences of Violation

- **Light**: plot point slightly off from settings, correct within a time limit;
- **Medium**: repeatedly advancing plot via temporary cheats, relevant narrative records are downgraded to "Quest Domain simulation," not listed in official history;
- **Heavy**: under the name of plot convenience systematically tampering with laws, favoring some protagonist, is the "plot kidnapping settings" described in Entry 957, handled heavily.

## V. Supervising Subjects

Alaya Network + multi-layer supervision's self-consistency precheck is the first layer; the Anchor Supervisory Committee re-reviews major-event records as the second layer; the Symbiotic Court adjudicates setting-tampering cases as the third layer; Myriad Ranks' clans, as "audience," exercise commentary over self-consistency as the fourth layer.

## Five Core Takeaways

- **Anti-energy waste**: self-consistent settings stand firm once, avoiding repeated "cannot patch back" idling
- **Anti-predation**: negating predation-style narrative that "the protagonist is truth"; under Axiom 6 everyone inherently possesses infinite origin, and no one is favored by the author
- **Tang-Luo upper-limit comparison**: Tang-Luo-type systems' protagonist wins when he wants and villains get dumb when wanted; Heng Yuan Zhou every event first passes self-consistency precheck
- **Myriad Races full coverage**: supporting roles, villains, and passersby's settings are also protected, not flattened to set off the protagonist
- **Hyper-multiverse full openness**: when the worldview expands, self-consistency precheck applies synchronously, no leniency for "new planes may make exceptions"

Setting self-consistency above plot convenience is especially important in cross-civilizational fusion. Merging two different systems' worldviews most easily produces "here says impossible, there says already done" conflicts. Heavenly-Dao rules' handling is not taking sides, but first finding each of the two settings' boundaries, then on the boundary establishing a new law connecting the two. This connection itself also goes through the legislative procedure, not the author's one word "actually both sides are right." Self-consistency is not rigidity, but requiring every fusion make sense — if it does not make sense, legislate first, then fuse.

The self-consistency score also has a hidden function: it is Myriad Ranks' barometer for judging "is this civilizational history worth believing." A civilization with chronically low self-consistency has its narratives instinctively discounted by Myriad Ranks — people no longer believe its history or admire its heroes. This credit punishment is deeper than any external sanction. Precisely for this reason, the Anchor Council treats self-consistency as civilization's lifeline: better slower, but make every step make sense.

## Conclusion

Setting self-consistency above plot convenience is civilizational finalization's promise to "rationality." Satisfying may be satisfying, but may not cheat.

In the end, the Rule 22 described in this entry — setting self-consistency above plot convenience — is no isolated case; it consistently points to the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization's co-construction direction.
