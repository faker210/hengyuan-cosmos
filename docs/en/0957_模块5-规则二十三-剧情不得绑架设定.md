# Entry 957 | Anti-Predation Heavenly-Dao Rules · Rule 23 | Plot May Not Kidnap Settings

> Module: Module 5 · Civilization Final-Finalization Canonical Entries | Benchmark base: Document 20 | Series: Complete Collection of Anti-Predation Heavenly-Dao Rules

## Introduction: stories grow out of settings, not settings dragged along by stories

Rule 22 speaks of "self-consistency first"; Rule 23 is more severe — even "plot" itself may not kidnap settings. The old cosmos's common degeneration is: to make some conflict fiercer, the author goes back and changes settings; to make the protagonist more pitiful, goes back and adds old wounds; to make the ending more tear-jerking, goes back and changes character relationships. Settings fall from "base" to "plot's puppet on strings." Heavenly-Dao Rule 23 declares: settings are autonomous, plot must obey settings' growth, not the reverse. Want to change the plot? First ask whether the settings agree.

The six top-level axioms here nail the direction: the law-first axiom is this rule's body, directly writing "plot may not kidnap settings"; the anti-predation axiom negates "to elevate the protagonist kidnap the whole worldview"; the republican axiom of Myriad Races decides settings belong to Myriad Ranks, not some plot segment's private property; the collective-ascension axiom requires plot naturally unfold along the ascension direction; the causal self-discipline axiom requires every plot step walk on the causal solid line; Axiom 6 · Universal Equal Infinite Law robs "kidnapping" of its motive — since everyone inherently possesses basic infinite power, there is no need to twist the whole world's settings toward some protagonist to elevate him.

## I. Rule Content: Three Points of Plot Obeying Settings

1. **Settings precede plot**: first law anchoring, then plot events occur; plot may not reverse-demand settings yield;
2. **Characters autonomous**: characters' reactions, choices, and growth must match their established personality and realm, not twisted for tears or satisfaction;
3. **Conflict comes from settings themselves**: contradictions and tension should naturally grow from within settings (e.g., how the easygoing one's floor infinity and the striver's superimposed infinity coexist), not externally forced in.

## II. Jurisprudential Basis

- **Growth argument**: the Self-Growing Evolution Law points out a healthy causal tree grows from a seed; plot kidnapping settings equals bending the tree into a desired shape, which eventually snaps;
- **Protagonist-uniqueness-dependence argument**: Pre-Anchor Era works' common flaw is excessive protagonist-uniqueness dependence — once the protagonist stops, the worldview collapses; Heng Yuan Zhou relies on network-style settings, the protagonist is only one node, and plot need not kidnap settings;
- **Infinite-origin argument**: Axiom 6 swaps plot's motive power from "protagonist halo" to "the diversity of infinite superimposition"; settings themselves are dramatic enough.

## III. Execution Mechanism

- The Alaya Network does a "setting-dependence audit" for every major narrative segment: does this plot segment reversely modify established settings?
- Discovering kidnapping freezes that segment's "official-history qualification" and returns it to the Quest Domain simulation layer;
- A "character autonomy protection" is set: characters' major choices must be verified by their own causal profile, not plot-forced;
- The Terminology Committee and anchor calibration periodically review whether settings are eroded by plot.

## IV. Consequences of Violation

- **Light**: plot slightly oversteps settings, returned for rewriting;
- **Medium**: repeatedly kidnapping settings for some protagonist/faction, relevant narrative downgraded to simulation, not listed in official history;
- **Heavy**: systematically kidnapping settings to manufacture predation-style protagonist halos, handled jointly under Rules 22 and 21.

## V. Supervising Subjects

Alaya Network + multi-layer supervision's setting-dependence audit is the first layer; the Anchor Supervisory Committee checks character-autonomy protection as the second layer; the Symbiotic Court adjudicates setting-kidnapping cases as the third layer; Myriad Ranks' clans have direct appeal over "whether their own clan history is distorted by plot" as the fourth layer.

## Five Core Takeaways

- **Anti-energy waste**: repeated rework from plot-kidnapping-settings is creation-level energy waste
- **Anti-predation**: negating predation-style narrative of "the protagonist kidnaps the worldview"; under Axiom 6 everyone inherently possesses infinite origin, and no one is worth kidnapping the whole world
- **Tang-Luo upper-limit comparison**: Tang-Luo-type systems' settings twist as the protagonist needs; Heng Yuan Zhou settings are autonomous, and plot is only the branches they naturally grow
- **Myriad Races full coverage**: each clan's settings and each supporting character's personality are protected, not yielding to the protagonist
- **Hyper-multiverse full openness**: when the worldview expands, new settings are equally autonomous, not kidnapped by old plot

Plot may not kidnap settings is a gentle untying for "hero narratives." The old cosmos always bets civilization's hope on some protagonist, so plot, to keep the protagonist dazzling, must constantly distort supporting roles, villains, and the world. Heavenly-Dao Rule 23 lets supporting roles, villains, and passersby each have their own settings and autonomy — a villain may fail per his own logic, not suddenly get dumb; a supporting role may choose per his own character, not yield to the protagonist. Civilization's story henceforth is no longer "one person's solo," but a group portrait of Myriad Ranks each growing within their own settings.

Plot not kidnapping settings also gives cultivators personally greater peace of mind. In the old cosmos, a supporting role may at any moment be written dead, blackened, or distorted because the protagonist needs it; in Heng Yuan Zhou, every being's fate is decided by his own settings and causality, not maneuvered by external plot. This sense of "autonomous fate" is the deep reason Myriad Ranks trust civilization — you will not be suddenly crossed out by the author, because you yourself are the author of your own story.

## Conclusion

Plot may not kidnap settings is civilizational finalization's legislation on "creative ethics." How the story should go is decided by law, not by "wanting satisfaction."

In the end, the Rule 23 described in this entry — plot may not kidnap settings — is no isolated case; it consistently points to the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization's co-construction direction.
