## I. The Philosophical Foundation of the Ethics Guidelines

The Cross-Plane Ethics Guidelines are the moral norms and codes of conduct that the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization must follow when interacting with civilizations of other planes. Their philosophical foundation is the principle of "civilizational subjectivity" — every civilization of every plane, regardless of its level of development, racial form, or social system, possesses inalienable subjectivity and the right to autonomous development. No foreign civilization may deprive this subjectivity for any reason, or impose its own will upon other civilizations.

The establishment of this philosophical foundation is in the same vein as the "anti-predation" core concept of the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization. The essential characteristic of a predatory civilization is its refusal to recognize the subjectivity of other civilizations, treating other civilizations as resources to be exploited or objects to be conquered. For the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization to truly transcend predatory civilizations, it must thoroughly abandon this mindset in cross-plane interaction, treating every civilization it encounters with equality, respect, and symbiosis.

The scope of application of the Cross-Plane Ethics Guidelines covers all cross-plane interaction behaviors, including but not limited to: plane travel, cross-plane communication, archaeological activities, trade exchanges, cultural exchanges, technical cooperation, and humanitarian intervention in emergency situations. Any organization or individual conducting cross-plane interaction must comply with all provisions of these guidelines.

## II. The Six Ethical Principles

The Cross-Plane Ethics Guidelines consist of six fundamental principles, forming a complete ethical value system:

| Principle | Core Connotation | Specific Requirements |
|------|----------|----------|
| Principle of Civilizational Autonomy | Respect the autonomous development right of the target plane's civilization, do not forcibly interfere in its internal affairs | Must not forcibly change the target civilization's social system, ideology, or development path; must not cultivate agents or puppet regimes within the target civilization |
| Principle of Non-Interference | Unless the target civilization faces an extinction-level crisis and actively requests aid, no substantive interference is conducted | Must not intervene in internal conflicts of the target civilization; must not provide weapons or military aid to any party of the target civilization; must not manipulate the target civilization's public opinion and political processes |
| Principle of Cultural Respect | Respect the cultural traditions, religious beliefs, and customs of the target civilization | Must not make discriminatory evaluations or malicious mockery of the target civilization's culture; must not forcibly promote one's own plane's cultural products and values; must not carry out cultural assimilation in the name of "civilizing" |
| Principle of Equality and Mutual Benefit | Cross-plane interaction must be built on equality, ensuring both sides benefit from the interaction | Must not use technological advantages for unequal trade; must not plunder the resources of the target civilization; technology export must consider the target civilization's capacity to accept and social impact |
| Principle of Minimal Impact | Cross-plane interaction should minimize external impact on the target civilization | Prefer low-contact, low-interference interaction methods; the scope and intensity of interaction activities should be strictly controlled within necessary limits; after interaction ends, assess and eliminate unnecessary residual impacts |
| Principle of Accountability | All cross-plane interaction behaviors must be traceable, monitorable, and accountable | Establish complete interaction archives and logs; establish an independent ethics supervision institution; investigate and punish behaviors that violate the ethics guidelines |

There is an inherent logical connection among the six principles: the Principle of Civilizational Autonomy is the fundamental value, the Principle of Non-Interference is the core requirement, the Principle of Cultural Respect is the cultural manifestation, the Principle of Equality and Mutual Benefit is the economic safeguard, the Principle of Minimal Impact is the operational norm, and the Principle of Accountability is the institutional constraint. Together, the six constitute a complete ethical closed loop of "value — requirement — manifestation — safeguard — norm — constraint".

## III. Ethical Norms for Different Interaction Scenarios

### 3.1 Ethical Norms for Archaeological Scenarios

Cross-plane archaeology is one of the most common interaction scenarios, and the core of its ethical norms is "non-disturbance". The archaeological team must strictly follow the standard scheme of Replication Mirror + Isolated Space + Knowledge-Only Retrieval, ensuring that the archaeological process is not perceived by the target civilization. For planes where an active civilization still exists, the scope of archaeology must be strictly limited to abandoned ruin areas, and no form of scanning or replication of the living areas of the active civilization is permitted.

When the knowledge discovered by archaeology is retrieved and published, the cultural sensitivity of the target civilization must be respected. If the archaeological findings involve religious relics, ancestral remains, or cultural taboos of the target civilization, the relevant information must undergo a cultural sensitivity review by the Cross-Plane Ethics Committee before publication, and sensitive details should be blurred when necessary.

### 3.2 Ethical Norms for Communication Scenarios

Cross-plane communication is a means of direct exchange with the target civilization, and the core of its ethical norms is "sincerity and respect". The content of communication must be truthful, without deliberately providing false information or deceptive propaganda; the tone of communication must be equal, without treating civilizations at lower development levels with a condescending attitude; the themes of communication must be agreed upon by the target civilization, without forcibly promoting one's own plane's ideology or religious beliefs.

When establishing communication with an unknown civilization for the first time, the "gradual contact" norm must be followed: first send neutral mathematical and physical signals to confirm the other party's level of intelligence and communication ability, then gradually expand the scope of exchange based on the other party's response; complex cultural or technical information must not be sent during first contact.

### 3.3 Ethical Norms for Trade Scenarios

The core of the ethical norms for cross-plane trade is "fairness and sustainability". Trade must be based on the voluntary consent of both sides, without using information asymmetry or technological advantages for coercive transactions; trade prices must be fair, without predatory pricing; trade goods must not include content that may cause social shock or ecological damage to the target civilization.

Special attention should be paid to the fact that cross-plane trade in cultivation technology and high-energy weapons is subject to the strictest ethical restrictions. Exporting high-level cultivation technology to civilizations at lower development levels may lead to the imbalance of their social structures and the intensification of internal conflicts; exporting weapons may directly trigger wars. Therefore, these two types of trade are prohibited in principle, and may only be conducted after approval by a plenary session of the Anchor Assembly under extremely special circumstances.

### 3.4 Ethical Norms for Emergency Intervention Scenarios

When the target plane's civilization faces an extinction-level crisis (such as asteroid impact, super plague, law collapse), the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization may conduct humanitarian intervention. But even in such emergency situations, the following ethical norms must be observed:

- Before intervention, the informed consent of the target civilization must be obtained as much as possible; if the target civilization cannot respond effectively due to the crisis, the Cross-Plane Ethics Committee makes a "presumed consent" judgment on its behalf
- The intervention means must choose the scheme with the least impact on the target civilization, preferring indirect intervention (such as changing the asteroid's orbit) over direct intervention (such as direct arrival for rescue)
- During the intervention, no opportunity may be taken to obtain the resources or technology of the target civilization
- After the intervention ends, immediate withdrawal is required, leaving no permanent presence on the target plane
- Afterward, a complete intervention report must be submitted to the Cross-Plane Ethics Committee for ethical review

## IV. Ethics Supervision and Violation Handling

The Hengyuan Universe established the "Cross-Plane Ethics Committee" as an independent ethics supervision institution, responsible for formulating and interpreting the Cross-Plane Ethics Guidelines, approving high-risk cross-plane interaction projects, accepting ethics violation complaints, and investigating and adjudicating violations. The committee is composed of ethicists, jurists, scientists, and civilization representatives from different races and professional backgrounds, ensuring the diversity and fairness of decision-making.

For behaviors violating the Cross-Plane Ethics Guidelines, depending on the severity of the circumstances, the handling measures include: verbal warning, written reprimand, suspension of cross-plane interaction qualifications, revocation of travel permits, fines, imprisonment, and in extreme cases, "plane exile" (deprivation of the right to reside in the main planes of the Hengyuan Universe). All ethics violation handling results are recorded in the personal or organizational ethics files, permanently preserved.

## Conclusion

The Cross-Plane Ethics Guidelines are a concentrated manifestation of the moral height of the Anchor Symbiotic Civilization. They not only stipulate "what must not be done", but also proclaim "what should be become" — a truly great civilization is not one that has conquered many other civilizations, but one that has respected the subjectivity of many other civilizations. On the road of the Hengyuan Universe toward interstellar symbiosis, the Cross-Plane Ethics Guidelines will always be the moral lighthouse guiding the direction.
